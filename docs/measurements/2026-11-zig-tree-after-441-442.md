# The Zig tree read after komira-ai/tierstore#441 and komira-ai/tierstore#442, re-measured on the farm

The two targets of `measure/zig-toolchain` (komira-ai/tierstore#440) were run again on 2026-11-25, 14:00-18:30Z. The farm was taken
to run a release with both fixes. That is inferred, not measured: see "The release each node ran".

The tree is the Zig 0.12.0 toolchain: 15,189 files and 306,729,395 bytes in 988 directories, the writer's totals in its standard
error. The two fixes:
- komira-ai/tierstore#441 lets the writer's node fill a file from the pack it holds whole.
- komira-ai/tierstore#442 makes a range fill read four chunks at once and spreads a farm Mac's NFS handles over its node hash.

**Answer.**
- **Mac cold fetch.** The farm Mac's cold input fetch dropped: its median is 17.54 s, against 39.5 s before.
- **Writer's-node read.** It did not drop: 11.38 s median (n=3), against 10.61 s (n=2) before. At the same node load its two first
  reads (10.24 and 11.38 s) are below three cold reads (10.77-13.87 s), but the ranges overlap. It is not near a warm read.
- **Mac slow mode.** A farm Mac's input fetch has a slow mode, about 30 s longer, that also hits warm reads.
  komira-ai/tierstore#442's earlier run already had warm fetches up to 30.1 s, so this run does not show the slow mode to be new.

What is still slow is filed as komira-ai/tierstore#474. No code changes here.

### At the same load

The 1-minute load average of the reader's node, in its report, rose from 20-33 for the first readers to 60-174 for the later ones. The
farm was full all afternoon; that is inferred from the queue. buck2's `queue_time_us` of each action, in its event log, was 19.3-22.0
minutes for the Linux writers and 32.9-33.8 minutes for the first Linux readers. For the Mac actions it was 0-16 s.

Only the first readers of each tree ran at a load comparable to the runs before, and the earlier load is not recorded. Those, under a
load of 40:

| writer's node | 10.81 [10.24-11.38], n=2 |
| cold | 12.80 [10.77-13.87], n=3 |

So on the writer's node the read is about 16% shorter than a cold read at the same load, on two and three runs whose ranges overlap.

- **Warm reads.** The warm reads of this run are not comparable with the 1.60 s before: they all ran at a load of 108-174.
- **Cold reads.** At a load under 40 they took 12.80 s, against 9.24 s before. That is not explained: the load of the earlier runs is
  not known.

## Where the time goes now

These are not measured; each is inferred from the numbers above.

- **Linux, writer's node.**
  - The read costs about what a cold read costs: about 0.75 ms of its wall time per file (11.38 s over 15,189 files, on 16 threads).
  - The second pass, on the same mount, is 1.3 s.
  - So the time is in the first fill of each file. These numbers do not say whether the bytes came from a held copy or from the
    store.
  - Whether the writer's node took the held-copy path at all is not known. The keep of the pack is best effort, and no node could be
    sampled.
- **Farm Mac.**
  - The fetch takes one of two modes: 6.1-17.5 s or 24.8-41.4 s. That holds in writers, cold, warm and unclassified runs alike.
  - One job at a time on one Mac, tree M7 was read in 9.73 s and, 19 s later, again in 39.32 s.
  - Tree M5 was read cold in 11.39 s and then warm in 37.78 s.
  - The slow mode is about 26-30 s longer than the fast one. That points to a wait of about 30 s somewhere in the input fetch, such as
    a timer, a poll or a timeout, not to the bytes of the tree.

The samples komira-ai/tierstore#442 took could not be taken. Those were `/metricz` and `nfsstat -c` on the Macs, and node metrics. The
section "Not measured" says why. komira-ai/tierstore#474 holds these numbers and what would settle each.

## How each run was classified

- **Each job's node.** It is read from the job service's log, read-only. Its line
  `a job the history sized was placed job=<job id> host=<node>` names the node.
- **Linux readers.** Each reader's report names its own job id, in the mount line of `node`: the job's root is mounted from
  `.../mounts/js-<lease>-<job id>/...`.
- **Writers and Mac jobs.** A Mac report has no `node`, since macOS has no `/proc`, and a writer has no report.
  - Each of these was matched to the job of its tool's class whose end is nearest the last instant of its ActionResult. The end is the
    log line `a run was checked against its class's profile`.
  - The matching was one to one, closest pairs first, within 5 s for readers and 10 s for writers.
  - The distances were 0.12-0.61 s for Linux writers, 2.23-5.50 s for Mac writers and 0.18-1.70 s for Mac readers.
  - No two candidates were within 2 s of each other on different hosts.
- **The classes.**
  - The first read of the tree on the writer's node is "writer's node".
  - A first read of the tree on another node is "cold".
  - A later job on a node that already read the tree is "warm", the writer's node included. L1's read 4 ran on its writer's node and is
    warm.
- **Unknown.** The job service restarted at about 17:20Z, and its log of 17:10-17:25Z was gone after it.
  - The six reads of trees M8-M10 are therefore "unknown", and they are in no class.
  - Their fetches were 29.84, 12.12, 41.43, 8.33, 40.30 and 38.99 s.
- **Salted trees.** Every tree is salted with its own nonce, so its blobs were on no node before its writer ran (README, "The salt").
  The five Linux trees have five different digests.

## Commands

The README's commands, from the repository root, with `ts_zig.salt` added, and the `_macos` targets of `BUCK`. Each tree has a new
write nonce, used as its salt too, and each reader a new read nonce. The action cache was never bypassed.

```sh
buck2 build //measure/zig-toolchain:zig_tree -c ts_zig.write_nonce=<w> -c ts_zig.salt=<w> --materializations=none
buck2 build //measure/zig-toolchain:zig_tree_read -c ts_zig.write_nonce=<w> -c ts_zig.salt=<w> -c ts_zig.read_nonce=r<i>-<w> --show-output
buck2 build //measure/zig-toolchain:zig_tree_macos -c ts_zig.write_nonce=<w> -c ts_zig.salt=<w> --materializations=none
buck2 build //measure/zig-toolchain:zig_tree_read_macos -c ts_zig.write_nonce=<w> -c ts_zig.salt=<w> -c ts_zig.read_nonce=r<i>-<w> --show-output
```

- **Nonces.** `<w>` is `w-<tree>-<seconds>`, for example `w-L1-1795616369`, and `<i>` is the reader's number in its tree.
- **Parallel trees.** The five Linux trees and the first three Mac trees ran side by side.
  - Each had its own buck2 daemon (`--isolation-dir m-<tree>`), because a daemon runs one configuration at a time.
  - Trees M4-M12 ran one at a time, in one of those daemons.
- **Every summary line read local 0.** The lines, by count, including the trial pair's two builds:
  - 26 builds `Commands: 1 (cached: 0, remote: 1, local: 0)`;
  - 10 builds `Commands: 3 (cached: 2, remote: 1, local: 0)`;
  - 5 builds `Commands: 5 (cached: 0, remote: 5, local: 0)`;
  - 3 builds `Commands: 6 (cached: 0, remote: 6, local: 0)`;
  - 9 builds `Commands: 7 (cached: 6, remote: 1, local: 0)`.
- **Builds with no summary line.** Ten builds have none, and each of their actions ran later, in a build that needed it:
  - the eight first writers of trees L1-L5 and M1-M3, stopped by a 90-minute client timeout in the queue;
  - one earlier writer of tree L1, stopped when the batch was restarted;
  - the trial's first writer, stopped by a 15-minute timeout.
- **The trial pair.** It came first and checked the method, and it is in no table or median. Its writer ran on node A and its reader
  on node C, cold: 12.04 s at a load of 31.6.
