# The read path on the farm, November 2026: GET counts, tree-ready, demand reads, amplification and the drives (T10b, NOW profile)

The farm validation of the read path of `docs/design/fetch-cache-serve.md` (row T10b of section
18, and 17.3), run against the farm as it is: six combined nodes of the NOW profile (section
21.1), five of them reachable, with SeaweedFS as the store. The store of section 10 is not built
(only its design exists), so every figure of section 10 that T10b was to check (the drive
scheduler of 10.7, `LOCATE`, hedges to a second hot replica, degraded reads, the audit of
stripes) has no subject here; where SeaweedFS has a counterpart it is measured and named as
such.

Real job trees, as the store holds them today, were replayed through the read path the daemon
will host: the Prepare planner (`tierstore-plan`), the fetch engine (`tierstore-fetch`), the
budget (`tierstore-budget`), the hash pool (`tierstore-hashpool`) and the Linux file entry store
(`tierstore-entry`), fetching each tree into a cache directory with every cell verified against
the tree's L2 list, or with no cell verified (below). The same trees were also fetched the way
the shipped daemon fetches them, for comparison. The harness is the crate `readbench`; its
records are in `crates/readbench/data/2026-11-t10b/`, and every table below is what
`readbench report` makes of them (a test holds this document to them: `cargo test -p readbench
--test results_doc`).

**In short.**

- **GET count per tree.** The engine sends one GET per part: 65 for a source tree of 18,120
  files in 20 packs, where the shipped daemon's way sends 18,193 (one per file and 8 MiB piece),
  and 30 against 49 for a tree of 13 large files. A tree whose files are stored alone (a loose
  blob each, `cas/l/<sha256>`) costs a GET per blob either way: the root file system images of
  the farm's jobs hold about 2,000 loose blobs each, so they take about 2,100 GETs on the engine
  against about 20,300 the shipped way. Over the 31 cold trees of a 31-client run the median is
  2,006 GETs per tree on the engine against 15,146 the shipped way.
- **Tree-ready under 31 clients.** With 31 clients on five nodes fetching 31 different trees
  nothing had read (13 to 15 GiB in all), the engine with 4 permits per drive had every tree
  ready at p50 3.8 s and p99 8.6 s; the shipped way took p50 9.4 s and p99 15.3 s. With the NOW
  profile's starting value of 1 permit per drive the engine's p99 was 25.9 s (its p50 5.1 s):
  the one-stream rule of the SMR drives, made for a store that admits one stream at the drive
  (10.7), throttles the client against a store that serves almost every read from memory.
- **Demand reads** (a job reading a span of 1 MiB or less of a pack nothing had fetched): wait
  p50 1.5 to 5.1 ms and p99 10.8 to 50.6 ms idle; under 31 clients p50 2.2 to 6.7 ms and p99
  12.2 to 183.9 ms. The idle target of section 1 (under 60 ms for a cold pack) holds; the store
  served those reads from its page cache, so this is not a measurement of a cold hard drive.
- **Amplification** (bytes fetched over bytes the tree's files hold), over 300 trees of the
  store: 1.08 in aggregate with the design's whole-pack constants, under the 1.5 of section 6,
  and 1.07 with any of four stricter variants. The per-tree tail (p99 20.8, largest 79.0) is
  the 1 MiB cell of section 2 on trees of a few small files, which no whole-pack constant
  changes. The whole-pack constants stay as they are.
- **The store's drives were barely read.** Over every 31-client run the ten SMR drives of the
  five nodes together read 0.1 to 23.3 MiB/s, while the clients fetched 12 to 17 GiB per run:
  the volume servers' page cache (about 190 GB of each node's 251 GB) held nearly all of it,
  for trees written in the last two days and never read by this harness. Per-drive queues on
  the hard drives stayed short (aqu-sz p99 0.1 to 7.4). The node SSD that holds the caches was
  the busy device under the engine (util p99 93 to 97%).
- **The shipped read path on the nodes** sent 2.8 to 5.8 GETs/s per node over the windows,
  one per fill, and `tierstore_request_to_start_seconds` gained no sample on any node: the
  farm's jobs reach their trees by lookups of `trees/<digest>`, not through the mount provider's
  `Prepare`, which is where that metric starts (inferred from the counters: the fills grew, the metric did not).
- **Not measured, and why** (each is named again below): the cache device of section 11 (the
  nodes still cache on the loop image; the direct-I/O step and the partition of T13 are not
  applied), so the constants of the cache device are not frozen; the first-touch report of 17.3
  and D state under `tsfuse` (no job reads a `tsfuse` mount before T-R); 31 build nodes (31
  clients ran on five nodes); a cold hard drive (above); the store's own scheduler, hedges and
  audit (not built).

| Store | SeaweedFS 4.54 on every node: a master (three of them), a filer with its S3 front, and a volume server over the two SMR drives; replication `001` (two copies on different nodes); 49 volumes, each with 2 copies (`readbench audit`); files cut in chunks of at most 64 MiB |
| Shipped read path | tierstore 0.3.147 on every node: the cache on xfs on a loop device over a 210 GiB image on the root SSD (direct I/O off), `tierstore fs` on the fuser fork; it reads the store through the filer's S3 front |

**How a tree is stored today, and how it was replayed.** A tree is a JSON manifest per
directory (`cas/l/<sha256 of the manifest>`); each file names its bytes as an offset in a pack
(`cas/b/...`), or, with no `source`, is a loose blob, the whole object `cas/l/<sha256 of the
file>`. No L2 list exists in the store yet (the CAS service's dual emission, T-K1a, is in no
release). `readbench` walks a tree's manifests (16 at a time, as `tierstore fs` does), encodes
the tree's version 2 list, makes its L2 sidecar from the packs' cells, and plans it with every
pack read from a hard drive (`Medium::Cold`). The engine's parts go straight to the volume
server holding each replica of a pack's chunk, so a device of the per-drive budget is one SMR
drive of one node (`(node, disk)`), and a chunk of a pack is one extent with two copies, the
shape of a chain object of the design (10.2). SeaweedFS reports no queue wait or drive state,
so the budget's signals are its first-byte and network times only (4.3).

**Verified and unverified runs.** Making a tree's L2 sidecar reads every pack once, which loads
the packs into the volume servers' page cache. So two kinds of run were made:

- *Verified* (the runs named `idle-*`, `load31-*`, `sweep-*` and `demand-idle-*`): five trees,
  their sidecars made first, every cell hashed by the hash pool (3 threads, sized by the pool's
  own rule at startup) and checked against its digest. Every such run read packs the volume
  servers had just served.
- *Unverified* (`cold-*`): trees nothing of this harness had read, planned from the packs'
  sizes alone, and fetched as `digest=none` objects (section 9): no cell hashed, every GET
  pinned to the pack's ETag. Each tree was fetched by one client only, so each first read is
  the store's.

**The shipped way** (mode `shipped`) is the shipped daemon's fill, as `readbench` reproduces it:
one ranged GET through the filer per 8 MiB piece of each file's span (`fill_chunk`), at most 16
in flight (`fill_socket_concurrency`), each file hashed as it arrives. Its bytes are not
written, so its tree-ready time leaves out the cache writes the engine's includes.

**31 clients.** The 31-client runs started 7 clients on one node and 6 on each of the other
four at one wall-clock time; each client has its own budget sized for 31 clients, its own
engine and its own cache directory. Six or seven clients share a node's network link and SSD,
which 31 build nodes would not.

## What was not measured, and what it waits for

- **The shipped cache device (section 11).** Every node still caches on xfs over a loop device
  on an image on the root SSD, with direct I/O off. T13's runbook has the direct-I/O step for
  the loop (a held step that needs a drained node) and the partition (by re-provision); neither
  is applied. The constants of the cache device (the dirty cap, the DONTNEED default, the
  journal's batch) are therefore not frozen here: a measurement of them on the loop would be
  replaced as soon as the device changes. The harness's caches lived on the same SSD as the loop
  image, on its root file system.
- **Prepare's order (17.3)** and **D state under `tsfuse`.** `first_touch_total` and
  `first_read_seconds` are counted by the tree mount on `tsfuse`, which serves no job until T-R
  switches the nodes, and T-R follows T10b in section 18. The first-touch report of 17.3 is
  therefore the first measurement after T-R, not part of this one. D state and FUSE waiting
  under a real build on the shipped mount are in `2026-11-fuse-results.md` (T-B4).
- **Request to start.** The mount provider's `Prepare`, where
  `tierstore_request_to_start_seconds` starts, is not on the path of the farm's jobs today
  (they look trees up by digest): the metric had no sample on any node.
- **31 build nodes.** 31 clients ran on five nodes (above).
- **A cold hard drive.** The volume servers served nearly every read from memory; a pack that
  must come from an SMR drive was not found among the trees of the store's last two days.
- **The store of section 10** (its drive scheduler and constants, `LOCATE`, hedges, degraded
  reads, the stripe audit and its histograms): not built. SeaweedFS's counterpart of the copy
  audit is the table above (49 volumes, 2 copies each).

## Found on the way

- **A descriptor per loose blob.** The file entry store keeps a data file and a journal open,
  and a state memfd, per entry; a root file system tree of about 2,000 loose blobs opens about
  4,100 files at once. Under the shell's default limit of 1,024 descriptors the engine's fill
  failed, and the failure reached the waiter as a cell `eio`, not as the entry store's error
  (the run was repeated with a higher limit). The daemon's units allow 65,536, which a node
  fetching more than about 16 such trees at once would also pass.
- **First bytes of large parts.** The engine's per-GET first byte p50 stayed under 5 ms, and its
  p99 reached 0.6 to 2.6 s on trees of large packs (64 MiB parts); the shipped way's p99 on the
  same trees reached 0.7 to 3.6 s. Inferred, not measured: the volume server reads a chunk's
  needle before it answers a range of it.

## Raw data and how it was made

The records are in `crates/readbench/data/2026-11-t10b/`: `runs.jsonl` (one `ClientRun` per
client), `demand.jsonl`, `plans.jsonl`, `disks.jsonl` (the SSD and SMR drives of each node; the
nodes' loop devices were sampled and left out) and `metrics.jsonl` (the series of the table
above). `cargo build --release -p readbench`, then on the nodes, with the store's filer at
`192.0.2.21:8888` and the volume servers named `<node id>=<host:port>`:

```
readbench prepare --filer http://192.0.2.21:8888 --bucket <bucket> --volume-server 2=192.0.2.22:8080 ... --tree <root> ... --sidecars DIR
readbench replay --filer http://192.0.2.21:8888 --bucket <bucket> --volume-server ... --tree <root> ... --sidecars DIR --cache DIR \
    --mode engine --clients 6 --total-clients 31 --client-base 6 --node n3 --run load31-engine-ds4-r1 \
    --start-at <wall ms> --drive-streams 4 --out runs.jsonl          # add --unverified for a cold tree
readbench demand ... --tree <root> --probes 20 --load-clients 31 --out demand.jsonl
readbench disks --devices sda,sdb,sdc --interval-ms 1000 --count 150 --run <run> --node n3 --out disks.jsonl
readbench scrape --metrics n3=http://192.0.2.23:9300/metricz ... --window-ms 150000 --run <run> --out metrics.jsonl
readbench sample-trees --filer http://192.0.2.21:8888 --after 4 --scan 300000 --since 2026-11-19 --min-bytes 200 --min-dirs 3 --count 300
readbench plan --filer http://192.0.2.21:8888 --trees-file trees.txt --variant design=50,64,20 ... --out plans.jsonl
readbench report --runs runs.jsonl --demand demand.jsonl --disks disks.jsonl --metrics metrics.jsonl --plans plans.jsonl \
    --class hdd=n2:sdb,n2:sdc,n3:sdb,n3:sdc,n4:sda,n4:sdc,n5:sda,n5:sdc,n6:sda,n6:sdc --class ssd=n2:sda,n3:sda,n4:sdb,n5:sdb,n6:sdb
```

The ssh sessions that started these on the nodes, and the order of the runs, are not part of
the repository; each record names its run, node and client.
