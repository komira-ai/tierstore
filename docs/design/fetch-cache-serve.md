# Fetch, cache and store: per-cell packs, tsfuse and a two-tier storage cluster (replicated NVMe hot tier, erasure-coded HDD cold tier)

Status: **proposed, for the owner's review (revision 7); not built, and nothing in it was run.** This document is the design of the farm's read path and of the store behind it: how a build node fetches the packs a tree names, keeps them in its cache, serves them to jobs through a FUSE mount (an NFS front on macOS), and where the packs live when no build node has them. Target hardware (owner, 2026-11-18): **31 build nodes**, each with a local SSD cache running the tierstore cache and tierstore-fs, and **3 storage nodes**, each with one 6.4 TB U.2 NVMe and fifteen 6 TB SAS hard drives (45 hard drives in all). The storage nodes run tierstore's own store: a small, replicated **hot tier** on the NVMe and a large **cold tier** of erasure-coded 1 GiB contiguous blocks on the hard drives, redundancy of each tier set by configuration. Tierstore owns this store end to end; it does not run on silo or SeaweedFS. It replaces the erasure-coded store "silo" (section 10). It is written as the system reads once built; each task of section 18 makes part of it true and states so in its pull request. What it says about today was read in the code of `main` and measured on the farm before this revision. It does not change [tierstore-design.md](tierstore-design.md) (the node cache, the entry protocol) or [put-without-lock.md](put-without-lock.md); it replaces the fetch and prefetch parts of [prefetch-api.md](prefetch-api.md) and [node-local-trees.md](node-local-trees.md) and the per-blob serving of [virtual-build-fs.md](virtual-build-fs.md) when the tasks of section 18 land (section 13 lists the code it deletes).

**What changed from revision 5** (the revision merged before this one, which put the cold tier on the build nodes' own hard drives): the cold tier now lives on three storage nodes, beside a replicated NVMe hot tier. Sections 1 to 9 (the read path on the build nodes) keep their design and are updated for 31 clients reading from 3 storage nodes (4.3 has the budget arithmetic, including degraded reads). Section 10 is the storage tier: roles, units, hot tier, cold tier, movement, metadata, configuration, capacity, the SeaweedFS, Ceph and silo comparison and the migration. Sections 11 to 20 are updated to match. Revision 6 (not merged) introduced the storage tier; this revision 7 adds, after a critique of revision 6:
- **The owner's decisions on the LATER cluster:** cold code `4+4` and three hot replicas (every storage node holds every hot object, and a hot read is served by the least-loaded node without a hop); their consequences are in the decision table, 10.3, 10.6 and 10.15.
- **Deployment profiles (section 21):** one codebase and one schema serve today's six combined nodes (NOW: SATA SSD hot tier, `4+2` over six nodes beside silo on the SMR drives) and the target cluster (LATER), with a migration from NOW to LATER by configuration and membership only.
- **Evidence map (section 22):** for every main choice, the published result it rests on or the named experiment that decides it.
- **Ordering by a cluster stamp (10.2).** Records, relocates and tombstones carry a hybrid logical clock stamp that every storage node can compare; a key is live when its newest fact is not a tombstone. A delete while the writing node is down, and a re-PUT of identical bytes after a delete, both resolve correctly.
- **One sealed length per segment (10.3).** A seal, including the early seal by the writer, is agreed by a majority of the three storage nodes before anything acts on it, so two sealers under a partition cannot leave two different sealed copies. Create-only is stated precisely: it is linearizable when the nodes' views agree, and a divergence is counted, not prevented (10.5).
- **Silo retires only after repair exists (10.11, 18).** Milestone S1 now includes hot and cold-copy re-replication, scrub of copies and the margin and live-key audits; erasure-code rebuild stays in S2.
- **Degraded reads in the budget (4.3, 10.4).** A degraded part is charged `k` stream slots; capacity with one node down is stated (about 2.8 GB/s against 8.5 GB/s healthy) and measured with a pass bar (17.2).
- **Rebuild arithmetic corrected (10.4, 10.15).** It counts writes and one decode per stripe, uses the fill the capacity guard allows, and the guard keeps one drive of slack per surviving node; rebuild shards may exceed `fill_limit` up to a ceiling; margin 0 alerts and scrubs first while a node is down.
- **Stripe free has an owner and a commit (10.5).** Fewer open segments (one per replica pair and expiry class), an age seal only above a floor, stripes from any replica pair by similar length, a cold-fill efficiency metric, and a stated rule for objects near `block_bytes`.
- **The index is kept in an embedded ordered store (10.2)**, rebuildable from the segments; it is a cache of the bytes, and the crate's own durability is not relied on.
- **Units are explicit (10.2):** the 1 GiB block is the unit of storage, placement, erasure coding and destage only; the unit of read, fetch and promotion is the object or a byte range of it.
- **Destage is decoupled from eviction (10.5):** destage copies dirty data to cold; reclaim only drops clean data; promoted copies live in a separate read-cache area that the destager never reads.
- **A hot-only mode (10.14):** the same code without a cold tier, `on_full = "evict"` (a cache) or `"reject"` (507, never drop), with example configurations.
- **Corrected comparison (10.12):** SeaweedFS's erasure coding already uses 1 GB contiguous blocks; revision 5's reasons for not using SeaweedFS are withdrawn, and the reasons that remain are listed. Ceph RGW is added as a baseline.
- The first farm measurements of T0 (one SMR drive's sequential and scattered rates, sha256 speed without SHA-NI, the loop-device cache) are in the measured facts, and moving the cache off the loop device is now required (section 11).

## Decisions for the owner

**Decided by the owner (2026-11-18); this document is written against them.**
- Hardware: 3 storage nodes, each 1 x 6.4 TB U.2 NVMe and 15 x 6 TB SAS HDD; 31 build nodes with a local SSD cache.
- Tierstore owns the storage tier: no silo and no SeaweedFS underneath it.
- The hot tier is deliberately small against the cold tier (cost). **Hot tier: replication only** (for speed). **Cold tier: erasure coding over 1 GiB contiguous blocks** (a normal read touches one drive; parity is read only on failure), with replication as the alternative.
- Redundancy of both tiers is **configuration** (`replicas = N`, or `ec = k+m` with a block size, and a failure domain of node or drive), so other installs choose; an install may also run without a cold tier (10.14).
- Small objects must be supported, though not encouraged on hard drives: the hot tier packs them into append-only segments as they arrive, sealed at about 1 GiB. The cold tier receives only sealed immutable blocks, written sequentially.
- Movement is automatic and ours: seal, then destage to cold (decoupled from eviction); promote an object back to hot when it is read again.

**The owner's questions.** Q1 and Q2 are **decided by the owner (2026-11-18)**; Q3 and Q4 are open, with a default this document is written against. A different answer changes the sections named. Two deployments run the same code with different profiles (section 21): **NOW**, six combined build-and-storage nodes on today's hardware, and **LATER**, the three storage nodes and 31 build nodes; Q1 and Q2 are the LATER profile.

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
- Fetch every pack's needed cells exactly once per node, as ranged parts in parallel, into one cache entry.
- Serve files as spans of that entry. FUSE reader threads never wait on the network, a fill or a hash; metadata is answered from mapped memory.
- One fetch engine for demand reads, mount Prepare prefetch and S3-front misses of large objects; one engine on Linux and macOS.
- **One storage tier owned by tierstore**, on three storage nodes: a replicated NVMe hot tier and a cold tier of erasure-coded (or replicated) 1 GiB contiguous blocks on hard drives, so a normal read of any object, a 1 GiB pack included, is one sequential stream from one drive (hot: one NVMe file or record range; cold: one data shard). Redundancy of each tier is configuration (10.14). There are no erasure sets on the build nodes.
- Every wait is observable, and every degradation (a stalled drive, an under-replicated pack, a slow consumer) is counted where an operator looks.

**Launch budget.** Request to command start under 200 ms, 100 ms as the target to aim for.
- Reachable only when the command's first spans are local before the action asks; Prepare at job assignment does that.
- Demand-read first-byte target, idle farm: under 10 ms [I] for a span of 1 MiB or less in a pack on the hot tier, and under 60 ms for one in a pack on the cold tier (a seek of about 12 ms plus a 4 MiB first part at about 190 MB/s on one 7.2k SAS drive [I]; T0-S measures the drives). Under load a demand preempts at the next chunk boundary (10.7), which bounds its drive wait against lower classes to about one 4 MiB chunk plus two seeks plus its own chunk (about 100 ms [I]); against equal-class demands (a cold-start storm) the wait is a slice plus up to `queue_max` queued requests, so only the idle figure is a target. T10b measures both.
- **Launch before Prepare is done.** Nothing gates launch on Prepare. A read on cells not yet present is `demand`: it promotes its part and, if the part has not started, issues a demand sub-range GET (4.5). Each such read increments `launch_before_ready_total` and records `read_wait_seconds{cause="launch_before_prepare"}`.
- Warm open and read: no upstream traffic, no RPC and no lock wait.

**Non-goals**
- Content-defined chunking or dedup across packs.
- A cluster-wide limiter service: admission is at the drive (10.7) and clients do flow control against its signals.
- Changing the pack byte layout.
- Erasure coding or any durable data on the build nodes: they keep only their cache. Redundancy is the storage tier's (10.3, 10.4). The storage tier is durable and, with a cold tier (our cluster), never evicts for capacity (10.5); a hot-only install configured with `on_full = "evict"` is a cache by choice, and its evictions are misses (10.14); a pack lost beyond the configured redundancy is a miss that the CAS service republishes (3.5, 4.6).
- Kernel fscache/cachefiles; FUSE passthrough (deleted); any reader of v1 lists.
- A general S3 server: multipart, versioning, ACLs, tagging, copy and overwrite are not offered; create-only PUT, range GET, HEAD, DELETE and prefix LIST are (10.9).

## 2. Data model

**Identity**
- `PackId = (object key, pack_root)`. `pack_root` is computed by the reader from the list's cell digests (3.1); two lists naming the same pack bytes give the same id and a changed pack cannot alias an old entry. No HEAD is needed.
- S3-front large objects have no digests: `PackId = (key, ETag)` with `digest=none` (section 9). In the storage tier the ETag is the object's **generation id**: the 128-bit blake3 of the object's bytes, computed while they stream in (10.2). It is a function of the content, so every copy of an object holds the same generation, a retried or raced PUT of the same bytes is an identical PUT, and one object is one `PackId` whichever copy served it.
- One cache entry per `PackId`. Packs are immutable.

**Entry on disk** (cache filesystem, section 11)
- Data: `packs-v2/aa/bb/<hash(key, pack_root)>.pack`, a **sparse** file (`ftruncate` to the pack size, never `fallocate`; space is accounted by present cells).
- Journal and snapshot: `<hash>.jnl` (append-only) and `<hash>.meta` (snapshot, atomic rename). Section 8.
- `flock(LOCK_EX)` on the data file is held by the owning tierstore process.

**Cell** = 1 MiB of pack bytes on a grid anchored at pack offset 0 (the last cell may be short). It is the digest unit, the readiness unit and the minimum fetch unit. Cell state is one byte: **low 3 bits state, bit 7 `durable`** (set by the server after the batch `fdatasync` of section 8; meaningful only on `present`).

| State | Meaning | Transitions |
|---|---|---|
| `absent` | no bytes on disk (or not trusted) | to `claimed` by CAS |
| `claimed` | a fetch attempt owns it; bytes in flight | to `present` (verified and written) or back to `absent` (attempt failed) |
| `present` | verified bytes on disk, servable by the mount without RPC | to `evicting` by CAS only |
| `recheck` | bytes are on disk from before an unclean start in another boot (section 8); re-hashed before serving | to `present` (hash matches) or `absent` |
| `evicting` | the evictor owns it; readers must not use it | to `absent` (punched) or back to `present` (evictor timeout) |
| `bad` | failed verification twice; EIO until expiry (60 s) | to `absent` |

- A cell is written once per residency. Only the claim owner writes it, only after its bytes hash to the table digest; CAS `claimed -> present` has exactly one winner, so a hedge or retry cannot publish twice.
- **Every transition is a CAS from a named predecessor state; no state is ever overwritten by a plain store.** The mount's RPC-free read path treats anything but `present` as "not ready".
- The state array lives in a shared memfd that mounts map read-only (5.1).

**Span**: `(pack, off, len)` from the list; a file is one span, or several if it crosses packs. Span readiness = all covering cells `present`. **Part**: the fetch unit (4.4): a cell-aligned run on the part grid, or a demand sub-range.

| Thing | Key |
|---|---|
| Entry | PackId |
| Readiness | cell index within the entry |
| Fill state (single flight) | PackId, in memory, sharded map |
| Hold | (PackId, holder id); holder = mount session or Prepare |
| Verification | cell index (digest from the cell table) |
| Storage-tier location | key, from `LOCATE` against the index of the key's storage nodes (10.2, 10.6) |

## 3. The tree list "L2": per-cell digests, no legacy reader

**Why.** One sha256 per file forces a whole file to be fetched and hashed before its first byte. Per-cell digests make any cell verifiable on arrival, in any order. **[D, Q3 default]** Readers accept only L2; no reader parses v1.

### 3.1 Layout
All integers little-endian, 8-byte-aligned sections addressed by `(off u64, count u64)`, fixed-size records, zero parse (the reader indexes into the mmap). T-FMT fixes the bytes and may keep the v1 layout of spans, ranges, strings and the file-sha table where it is already zero-parse, to limit writer churn:
- **Header, 128 bytes:** magic, `version`, `hdr_len`, flags, `default_cell_shift`, `cell_digest` (the digest function of CELLS: 1 = sha256, the only value this revision writes; a reader refuses a value it does not know with `ListVersionUnsupported`), `tree_id` (the identity the list is stored under, 3.5), a section table (packs, spans, ranges, strings, file shas, cells, directories), and a crc32 of the header.
- **PACKS: one 48-byte record per pack** (key string reference, plus):

| Field | Size | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `pack_size` | 8 | bytes |
| `cell_first` | 8 | index of this pack's first digest in CELLS |
| `cell_count` | 4 | ceil(pack_size / cell) |
| `cell_shift` | 1 | 20 |
| `pack_flags` | 1 | bit 0: pack fully covered by spans |
| reserved | 2 | zero |
| `upload_part_size` | 8 | **0 for every pack written to the storage tier** (single streaming PUT, no parts). Lists of packs uploaded to silo as multipart keep their recorded nonzero value; it is used only when a fill reads from silo (4.4) and ignored once silo is retired. |

- **CELLS: a flat table of 32-byte sha256 digests**, one per cell, pack by pack: `digest[cell_first + i] = sha256(pack bytes [i << shift, min((i+1) << shift, pack_size)))`.
- **Spans** keep `(pack idx, offset, len, file sha index)`; the per-file sha256 stays as the end-to-end content address used by `tierstore verify` and by non-pack objects.
- **Directory records are sorted by name bytes** (T-FMT verifies the current image order; sorting lets lookup binary-search the mapped records, 5.3).
- `pack_root = sha256("TSPACK1" || pack_size_le64 || shift_u8 || digest[0] || ... || digest[n-1])`, computed when a pack is first opened and cached per list.

### 3.2 How the CAS service computes them
1. While streaming a pack to the storage tier, the CAS service keeps a 1 MiB accumulator, hashes each full cell and appends the digest; the last cell is hashed short.
2. The CAS service records `pack_size` and `cell_shift`. **The CAS service uploads every pack with one streaming PUT** (HTTP `Content-Length` if the pack size is known when the upload starts, else chunked transfer encoding) to tierstore's S3 front (10.9). There are no multipart parts; packs written to silo before the cutover keep their recorded `upload_part_size`.
3. The list is finalized by writing PACKS and CELLS, then the header last. Per-file sha256 is still computed, so the CAS service's hashing CPU roughly doubles; T-FMT measures it (the hashing pipelines with the upload).
4. A deterministic-output test (same inputs, same bytes) covers the new sections.

### 3.3 How a reader verifies a cell on arrival
1. Part bytes stream into pooled 1 MiB cell buffers (a part is a sequence of whole cells).
2. A full buffer goes to the bounded hash pool (4.7), which compares sha256 with `CELLS[cell_first + i]`.
3. Match: `pwrite` at `cell << shift`, CAS `claimed -> present`, wake waiters. Bytes are verified before they touch the file, so nothing is re-read to be verified and no later write can replace verified bytes.
4. Mismatch: 4.6 (the refetch goes to the other hot replica, or to a degraded read of the cold stripe). A `digest=none` pack skips client verification (section 9).

### 3.4 What the reader does not do, and what the list's integrity rests on
No per-file hash on the read path: a file is correct if every covering cell is `present`. **What pins the list** depends on how the tree reference is derived, which T-FMT answers first (T-FMT Q1):
- If the reference pins the L2 list bytes, cell digests are authenticated by it.
- If it pins only the v1-canonical file table (the likely case, 3.5), cell digests are as trustworthy as the list object in the storage tier, which has the same trust as the pack bytes beside it, plus the header crc and an offline cross-check: `tierstore verify` re-derives per-file sha256 from cells and compares with the file-sha table that the tree id covers. A wrong L2 digest can make a good cell fail verification (a visible EIO or miss), and cannot make a wrong cell pass unless the attacker can also write the list object. This gap exists today for v1 spans and is not widened. Closing it is decision Q4 (default: leave it).

### 3.5 Rollout order, tree identity, refusal and rollback
**Tree identity [D].** The tree id does not change. The CAS service computes the id from exactly the bytes it hashes today (the v1-canonical serialization, computed in memory) and stores the **L2 list as an additional object under the same id** (a sidecar next to the v1 list). Action-cache keys that include tree ids are therefore unchanged and the format change invalidates no action-cache entry. **T-FMT Q1 is the first step (read only): how is the id derived today.** If it is not derived from list bytes, the sidecar is trivial; if it is derived from the v1 bytes, the CAS service keeps computing those bytes for the id only; if it cannot be preserved, the owner is told before anything ships that the cutover cold-starts the action cache.

**Dual emission for exactly one release.** The release that adds L2 makes the CAS service write, for every new tree, both the v1 list and the L2 sidecar. Old readers ignore the sidecar; new readers read only the sidecar. After every reader is new (`tierstore_build_info` on all nodes including the Macs), a following release of the CAS service stops storing v1 (the id is still computed from the canonical bytes in memory).

**Order.**
1. **The CAS service's dual emission ships first.** It is invisible to old readers. The CAS service also runs the id-preserving backfill (`l2-backfill`) over trees referenced in the last N days, rate limited, so recent trees have a sidecar before any reader needs it. The backfill reads the packs once to compute cell digests; T-FMT estimates its cost per TiB, and it runs off-peak.
2. **Readers roll node by node** under the one-live-update-at-a-time coordinator. On an updated node a tree with no L2 sidecar is a miss.
3. When all nodes are new and the sidecar coverage is complete (`l2_sidecar_missing_total` zero for 7 days), the CAS service stops writing v1.

**Refusal.** A new reader never parses v1. A tree with no L2 sidecar returns a distinct `ListVersionUnsupported` status to the caller (`list_refused_total{reason}`). **The CAS service treats that status as "not mountable through tierstore" and takes its miss path (materialize the tree from the cold tier without the mount, or rebuild and republish); it never fails the job because of it** (T-K1a confirms the miss path exists and builds it before step 2 if it does not).

**Rollback.** During the dual-emission release both lists are stored, so rolling readers back loses nothing. After v1 stops, rolling readers back makes trees unreadable (misses) until readers roll forward; the runbook (T13) says to fix forward. Wrong bytes are never possible in either case.

## 4. Fetch engine

### 4.1 Single flight
One `PackFill` per `PackId` in a sharded map, no lock across I/O. It owns the cell state array, the part queue, the claim table, the pack's storage-tier location (the hot copies or the cold data shard found, with the generation, 10.6) and the fill's **pinned generation**: the generation of the hinted copy, else of the first copy that answers, set once when the location is first resolved and never changed for the life of the fill. A silo-origin pack has no storage-tier location: its pinned generation is the ETag of its first response, which is issued alone (section 9), and no later part starts before it is set. Any reader, prefetch or S3-front miss calls `ensure(pack, cell_runs, class, rank)`, which attaches to the existing fill and raises priority; it never starts a second fill.

### 4.2 Claims
- A fetch attempt claims only the cells it is about to fetch by CAS `absent -> claimed`; `claimed` and `present` cells are skipped, so a part overlapping an in-flight demand sub-range splits into runs of still-absent cells.
- If the owner fails, its cells return to `absent` and a waiter's demand re-claims them.
- **Validation:** no cell is fetched twice except by retry after failure or verification mismatch. GETs are counted by kind (`grid_part`, `demand_sub`, `retry`, `hedge`); the acceptance bound is GETs <= grid parts + demand sub-ranges + retries + hedges, and `bytes_fetched == bytes_needed + wasted`.

### 4.3 Budget: per-drive flow control under per-storage-node and per-client ceilings
**What limits the storage tier.** Each hard drive serves at most `s*` sequential streams at a time (default 2, the server's `drive.streams`), admitted and ordered by that drive's scheduler (10.7); each NVMe is bounded by a queue depth (`hot.queue_depth`, default 64). Admission is **server-side and authoritative**. A part is one contiguous byte range of one hot file, one hot record range or one cold data shard on one device, so one part is one stream on one device; a healthy read has no striping and no erasure width (10.4). The client's budget is flow control against what each device and each storage node advertise, not a model of them.

**Quantities, stated separately.** With `D` = 45 cold hard drives on 3 storage nodes, `N` = 31 build nodes (clients), `s*` = 2, and `P` the current part size:

| Quantity | Formula | At D = 45, N = 31, s* = 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Cold stream capacity | `G = s* x D` parts | 90 parts |
| Per-client share | `share = ceil(G / clients_active)` parts (`clients_active` from the jobscheduler hint, default N if missing or older than 10 s) | 3 parts |
| Per-client floor | 1 part at depth 1 at `P_floor` = 16 MiB, plus the demand reserve | 16 MiB per client, 496 MiB across 31 clients |
| Per-client ceiling | `2 x max(share, floor)` parts until T0-S replaces it with the measured knee | 6 parts |
| Bytes in flight at share | `share x P` | 192 MiB per client and about 5.8 GiB across 31 clients at P = 64 MiB; 48 MiB per client at P = 16 MiB |
| Per-drive permits | `per_drive_max[d]`, 1 or 2 for a hard drive (a larger value for an NVMe, T0-S), **one value per drive** | 2 per drive when that drive is healthy |
| Per-storage-node permits | `node_permits[n]`, the parts in flight to one storage node, bounded by its advertised link and CPU | set from the measured knee; unbounded until T0-S |

The budget unit is the part, so a part size never exceeds the per-client share: share is at least one part whatever P is. A client's parts land on drives by the object's location, so the expected load at full share is `G / D` = `s*` streams per cold drive when reads are spread over the drives; hot reads go to the NVMe and are bounded by its depth. Overload is absorbed by the server queue (10.7), never by collapse. **With 31 clients and 90 stream slots, a cold storm of more than about three parts per client queues at the drives, which is the intended place for it to queue.**

**Degraded reads cost `k` slots [D].** A part served by a degraded read (10.4) reads the same range from `k` drives. The reconstructor admits it as `k` stream charges, one on each source drive's scheduler (10.7), and the client charges it `k` parts against its ceiling and one part against each source drive's permits (`LOCATE` and the reconstructor's response name the source drives, chosen by their advertised queues, 10.6). **With one storage node down** about a third of cold reads are degraded (the data shards on that node) and a third of the spindles are gone: drive-read load is 2/3 x 1 + 1/3 x 4 = 2.0 times the healthy load, carried by 30 drives instead of 45, so the cold read capacity falls from about 8.5 GB/s to about 8.5 x (30/45) / 2 = **2.8 GB/s** [E] (at `4+2` the same). Today's 0.5 MB/s is far below either; a cold-start storm of 31 nodes during a rolling update of a storage node is the case that meets it, and 17.2 item 5 measures it with a pass bar. There is no read drain before a planned reboot: the data shards on the node cannot be read anywhere else, so the budget's job is to charge degraded parts honestly and let the drive queues absorb the rest.

**Three levels of state [D].** The controller keeps **per-drive state**, **per-storage-node state** and a **client ceiling**:
- *Per drive d:* `per_drive_max[d]`, a baseline and a decrease state of its own, fed by signals that drive d produced (its 503s, its `x-ts-queue-wait-ms` and `x-ts-service-ms`, first-byte time of GETs it served). A 503 or a doubled queue wait from drive d decreases **only drive d's permits** (2 to 1, then pacing of d), moves d's replicas to the end of the read preference, and leaves every other drive's fills untouched. A hot pack that queues its drive therefore throttles that drive and nothing else.
- *Per storage node n:* `node_permits[n]`, decreased only by evidence about that node as a whole: more than a third of the drives of n that the client has traffic on in a decrease state at once, or the client's network time per MiB for parts from n above 2 x its baseline for two windows (n's link or CPU is the bottleneck, for example a node rebuilding). One storage node being slow does not slow reads from the other two.
- *Client ceiling:* the total parts in flight across all storage nodes, bounded by the table above and decreased only by client-wide evidence: the client's own network signal above 2 x its baseline for two windows (its link or CPU), or more than a third of all drives with traffic in a decrease state (the farm as a whole is loaded). One slow drive cannot hide inside a pooled median, because no median is pooled across drives.

**Parameters [I until T0-S]:** `s* = 2`, `per_drive_max` up to 2, P from 4.4.

**Floor is a rate floor.** Under a sustained decrease state a drive steps down its own ladder, one step per window: (1) `per_drive_max` 2 to 1; (2) pacing: a minimum gap between part starts to that drive, doubling per window up to 2 s. A storage node's and the client ceiling's ladders are: (1) fewer parts in flight; (2) P toward 16 MiB (64 -> 32 -> 16). P is one client-level value per size class (4.4), never per drive, so a hot replica and a cold shard share one part grid and parts stay interchangeable for hedges and failover. A client never stops (one part per 2 s per drive at worst) and the farm can back off below one stream per drive.

**Controller**
- Window `max(16 parts, 2 s)`, per drive and for the ceiling. Increase only with backlog (queued parts and the permits fully used); slow start (double toward the share, then +1 part per window); state persists across idle.
- **Signals, normalized per size class:** (a) network time per MiB = (last byte - first byte) / size; (b) first-byte time; (c) **the drive's own report**: every storage-node response carries `x-ts-queue-wait-ms` (time the request waited for a stream slot), `x-ts-service-ms` and `x-ts-drive-state` (10.7), so the controller sees each drive's queue directly instead of inferring it. Local write and hash time are excluded.
- Baselines: windowed minimum (10 minutes) of the median of each signal per size class, **per drive** for (b) and (c), per client for (a) and per storage node for the node level. Decrease of drive d: median of d's (b) or (c) above 2 x d's baseline for two windows, or any `503 Retry-After` from d -> d's permits x 0.75 rounded down, at least the ladder step; then a cooldown of one window with +-50% jitter. A response with `x-ts-drive-state: stalled` or `indexing` is not a load signal: the copy is skipped (4.6) and d's permits are not changed.
- Local back-pressure is separate: the dirty-byte cap (4.8).
- Topology is the membership document (10.8) and `s*`; there are no per-fill concurrency knobs.

**Demand reserve.** `max(1 demand part, 25% of the client ceiling)` is held back from `prepare` and `background`, and one permit of every drive is reserved for `demand` while that drive has queued demand. At the drive the demand class also preempts (10.7), so the reserve buys drive time and not only node bytes.

### 4.4 Part sizing and the part grid
A part is a contiguous run of one pack file on one drive read as a stream: it reads at the drive's sequential rate whatever P is, as long as it continues in ascending order; P trades request overhead against first-byte time for the waiting reader and memory. Starting values (T0-F replaces them):

| Pack size | First piece | Ramp | Steady P |
|---|---|---|---|
| <= 32 MiB | first-touch cells (>= 2, <= 4 MiB), then one GET for the rest | - | - |
| 32 MiB to 512 MiB | 4 MiB | doubling | 32 MiB |
| > 512 MiB | 4 MiB | doubling | 64 MiB |

- **Grid.** The steady grid is anchored at pack offset 0 with cell-aligned pitch P. **The ramp is defined relative to the grid:** it covers `[first-touch cell start, next grid boundary)` (or to the pack end) in doubling pieces of 4, 8, 16 ... MiB capped at P/2, the last piece trimmed to the boundary. After the ramp every part is a whole grid part. A first touch in the middle of a pack costs one ramp and no further runts; a first touch at offset 0 ramps 4+8+16+32 = 60 MiB and the next piece is trimmed at the 64 MiB boundary. Demand sub-ranges split grid parts into runs of absent cells; a run is a valid fetch unit.
- **No upload-part boundary exists in the storage tier** (10.2). Only when a fill's source is the legacy upstream (silo, 10.10) and the pack's `upload_part_size != 0` are parts also cut at those boundaries; a fill from the storage tier ignores the recorded value. The cut makes each silo drive read one part file per GET; this rule is deleted with silo (T11b).
- **First part small (first-byte lever).** At least 2 cells, at most 4 MiB: on an idle drive about one seek plus 4 MiB at the media rate. If Prepare entered with a plan, the first part is the plan's first run.
- **Adaptation.** P moves within {16, 32, 64} MiB. While backlog exists, 1 part in 8 is issued at the next size up (the probe arm). Per size class, goodput per in-flight byte and drive-reported service time are compared over 10 minutes; move up if the probe is >= 10% better at equal-or-lower p95 first byte, back down if >= 10% worse. P is a node-level value per size class; a node-ceiling decrease state also steps it down (4.3).
- **Striping one pack across hot replicas (optional, measured).** With 2 or more hot replicas holding the same generation, parts of a pack larger than 128 MiB may alternate between replicas on different nodes, so two NVMe devices stream one pack in parallel. A cold pack is one data shard on one drive and has nothing to stripe. T0-S arm C-3 compares one replica streaming against striping; default off unless it wins by >= 20% at equal p99.

### 4.5 Order of parts, demand sub-ranges, fairness across jobs
- Classes: `demand` > `prepare` > `background`. The class, job id and rank travel to the drive in request headers (`x-ts-class`, `x-ts-job`, `x-ts-rank`) so the scheduler (10.7) applies the same order.
- **Within a class: deficit round robin across jobs**, quantum one part of the current P, oldest job first on ties. **Within a job** the key is `(rank, offset)`, where `rank` is the first-touch rank of the first needed cell inside the part (profile order, else tree order).
- **Active-pack window.** At most 3 packs are partly filled per class per node; each pack's parts go in ascending offset order, so the drive sees ascending reads of one file.
- A `demand` on a part that has not started promotes it. If the needed span is 1 MiB or less (4 MiB with alignment) and the part has not started, the engine issues a **demand sub-range GET** of exactly the covering cells (1 to 4 cells), `demand` class; later grid parts skip the claimed cells.
- A demand inside a started part waits for that cell only (cells publish as they land).

### 4.6 Retries, stalls, hedging, verification failures, lost packs
- **Generation known before the first byte.** The location of a stored pack (`LOCATE`, 10.6) carries its generation and size, because both are in the index entry and in the record header or file name (10.2, 10.3). Every GET of a fill carries `If-Match: <the fill's pinned generation>` (4.1), so one fill never takes bytes of two generations, even for a `digest=none` object whose cells the client cannot verify. For a silo-origin pack the generation is the ETag of the first response, learned with the first part alone (section 9).
- **Copies of another generation are excluded per fill.** A copy that lists or answers (412) another generation holds other bytes under the key (a defect, 10.5): it is dropped from this fill's location, `store_copy_generation_mismatch_total` counts it, and parts go only to copies of the pinned generation. The fill fails only when no reachable copy holds it.
- **Stall detection.** After the first byte: no bytes for `max(500 ms, 8 x baseline net time per MiB x 1 MiB)`. **Before the first byte** a GET is not a stall because of time alone (a queued drive is normal under load): the pre-first-byte timeout is `max(2 s, 8 x first-byte p99 baseline of the size class)`, and a response that already said `x-ts-queue-wait-ms` is not a stall while the drive is making progress. A response or ping saying the drive is `stalled` (10.7) ends the GET at once. A stalled part is cancelled and retried, **on the other hot replica, or as a degraded (reconstructing) read of the cold stripe when the stalled device is the data shard's drive, else on a fresh connection**, up to 3 attempts, jittered backoff.
- **Stall retries are budgeted.** A retry waits for a permit like any part, at most one stall retry per drive per window, and while that drive is in a decrease state only transport-level failures (reset, connect error, node down, drive `stalled`) are retried at once; everything else waits for the state to clear.
- **Hedge** only for `demand` class and only when an alternative exists: a second hot replica of the same generation with a shorter advertised queue wait, or, for a cold range of at most 4 MiB, a degraded read from the other shards of the stripe when the data shard's drive is slower than its baseline (10.4) and the reconstructor's advertised queues have `k` free slots (a degraded hedge costs `k` charges, 4.3); neither device in a decrease state; at most one hedge permit per client. Trigger: no first byte after `max(30 ms, 2 x first-byte p50 baseline)`. The hedge shares the claim; whichever attempt first delivers a verifying cell publishes, the other's bytes are dropped, never written. A hedge goes to a different device, so it routes around one slow drive; with neither alternative a demand is never hedged.
- **Verification failure:** the claim is released and the cell refetched once as a demand sub-range **from the other hot replica, or by degraded read of the cold stripe, if there is one** (else the same drive on a fresh connection). A second mismatch marks that cell `bad` for 60 s: reads covering it return EIO, other cells are unaffected. The serving node is told (`x-ts-report: bitrot`); it quarantines that copy or shard and the copy is restored by repair or rebuild (10.3, 10.4).
- **A pack lost mid-job [D].** A running job's tree may name a pack that every copy then answers 404 for (the CAS service deleted it, or every copy was lost). The storage tier with a cold tier never evicts for capacity (10.5), so a held pack disappears only through a DELETE by the CAS service's GC or through loss beyond the configured redundancy. When it happens anyway: `LOCATE` (10.6) is asked once more of every live storage node, bypassing every negative cache; a hit is served. If the miss is **not definitive** (10.6 step 3: fewer than `N - hot.min_acks + 1` live storage nodes, none `indexing`, answered), the fill waits and retries with backoff for up to `store.unavailable_wait` (default 10 minutes, longer than a routine reboot), so a reboot of the node holding the only copy delays the job and does not fail it. If the miss is definitive, or still not definitive when that wait ends, the fill is **lost**: waiters of unsatisfied cells get EIO, the tree's mount is marked lost for every holder, `store_pack_lost_total{phase="mid_job"}` counts it, and the mount provider reports each job holding the tree as failed with the **retriable** cause `tree-lost` to the jobscheduler (T-J1), which requeues it. On the retry the CAS service finds the tree not present and takes its miss path (republish or rebuild). No wrong byte is ever served, and the job is retried rather than failed. One lost pack marks the whole tree lost on purpose: a tree with a hole cannot build correctly.
- No reachable copy because storage nodes are `down`, an index is `indexing`, or the only shard's drive is `stalled` and no degraded read is possible yet is a non-definitive miss and takes the wait of the bullet above, never an immediate EIO. Other pack-level failure after retries (verification failing on every copy, a copy set the location rules exclude entirely): waiters of unsatisfied cells get EIO; the entry stays resumable and the next read retries.

### 4.7 Hash pool and CPU
Dedicated bounded pool, not `spawn_blocking`. Threads = `ceil(1.2 x target_rate / core_sha_rate)`, with `target_rate` the cache write ceiling (about 500 MB/s) and `core_sha_rate` measured at startup (about 1.5 GB/s with SHA-NI [I]; about 0.29 GB/s on today's build nodes, which have none [M]). The implementation is `ring`, about twice the `sha2` crate on these CPUs [M]; at 0.29 GB/s per core a 500 MB/s cache write ceiling needs 3 hash threads. Queue bound 32 cells; when full, receive pauses (`part_hash_wait_seconds`). The tierstore service has a higher `cpu.weight` than job scopes. `recheck` cells are hashed by the same pool at lower priority. A paused receive is a slow consumer for the serving drive; the drive's read-ahead buffer and slot release (10.7) keep it from holding the spindle.

### 4.8 Memory, dirty pages and DONTNEED
- Parts stream into pooled 1 MiB cell buffers; in-flight memory is at most `ceiling + 32 MiB` per node. A part from a storage node is received over the network straight into these buffers (no second copy on the build node, which has no tier drives).
- **Dirty cap** `min(256 MiB, 2 x node ceiling)`: cells wait before `pwrite` when reached (`dirty_bytes`, `writeback_stall_seconds`).
- **DONTNEED is server-only and keyed to the `durable` bit.** The journal committer (section 8) sets `durable` on a cell after the batch `fdatasync`. Only the server issues `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)`, only on cell-aligned ranges of `durable` cells, because DONTNEED on a dirty page does nothing (the mount cannot know a cell is durable). Policies are arms of T0 B and the section 17 benchmark: (a) never, (b) at commit, (c) when the pack-file page cache exceeds a cap, oldest durable groups first. Default (a) until data says otherwise. Races: DONTNEED against a splice in flight is safe (the pipe holds the old, correct pages); against a concurrent `pread` it is a re-read (performance); against a refetch after punch it is the same bytes; it can drop a neighbouring file's pages in the same cell (thrash on shared packs, never correctness).
- Parked FUSE replies hold no buffer; a pooled buffer is taken when the cell is ready.

### 4.9 Connections
One pooled HTTP client with a per-host pool of at least `ceiling / smallest part`, keep-alive, no per-request handshake. Connections go to the three storage nodes' peer addresses on the private network (10.6); the pool is shared by the node's mounts.

## 5. Serving

### 5.1 State page, reader counters and the eviction protocol
`open-pack` returns the **data fd** and a **state memfd** over the unix socket (SCM_RIGHTS). The data fd guarantees the right inode if the path is unlinked or recreated; the mount never reopens by path.

**State memfd.** The server creates the memfd, maps it read-write, writes the cell state bytes (and group touch epochs), then applies `F_SEAL_FUTURE_WRITE | F_SEAL_SHRINK | F_SEAL_GROW` and `F_SEAL_SEAL` before passing it. [K] `F_SEAL_FUTURE_WRITE` forbids new writable mappings and `write(2)` while the server's existing writable mapping stays valid. The mount maps it `PROT_READ` only and cannot mark a cell `present` or alter another mount's data. A conformance test asserts that a `PROT_WRITE` mapping from the passed descriptor fails (T-F7, T1).

**Counter memfd.** Each mount (holder) creates **one counter memfd for its whole life**, maps it read-write, and sends it to the server at handshake; the server maps it read-only. Inside it the mount allocates a **region per open pack**: one u16 reader counter per 16-cell group (256 groups x 2 B = 512 B for a 4 GiB pack), and tells the server `(region offset)` in `open-pack`. There is no slot table and no cap per pack, so the most shared packs (toolchains) are never refused. A new mount process always has a new memfd, so a counter is never reused across processes and cannot be underflowed by a late teardown of an old one. The memfd grows (`ftruncate`) as the mount opens more packs; a region offset beyond the server's current mapping makes the server remap that holder's memfd at the new size before it acknowledges the `open-pack`, so the evictor never reads past its mapping.
- **The server never zeroes or drops a live holder's counters.** Liveness is a **lifeline**: at handshake the mount also sends one end of a `socketpair` that is held open for the process's whole life, separate from the multiplexed protocol connection (which may reconnect many times). The server polls the lifeline; only `POLLHUP` (the process exited or closed it) ends the holder: the server then unmaps that holder's counter memfd and drops its holds. A mount whose protocol connection flaps keeps its counters, its regions and its holds; it re-sends `open-pack` with the same holder id and gets the same regions back. A pidfd is not used because mounts run in other pid namespaces.

**Reader protocol (mount, no RPC), for each 16-cell group covering the range:**
1. `fetch_add(group counter, 1, SeqCst)`.
2. `load(state of every cell in the range, SeqCst)`: if any is not `present`, `fetch_sub`, then `wait` (or EIO for `bad`).
3. Read the bytes (pread, or splice into the pipe, 5.3).
4. **Post-read recheck:** reload the state of every covered cell (SeqCst); if any is not `present`, discard the bytes (empty the pipe or drop the buffer), `fetch_sub`, and go to `wait`. One extra load of the state bytes means even a forced counter loss cannot serve zeros.
5. Commit the reply; then `fetch_sub(group counter, 1, SeqCst)`.

**Evictor protocol (server), per cell, never per group range:**
1. For each candidate cell, **CAS `present -> evicting`**; remember exactly the cells won. A cell that is `claimed`, `absent`, `recheck` or `bad` is not touched, so an in-flight fetch's ownership is never overwritten, and a neighbouring cell going `absent -> claimed -> present` during the scan is simply not in the set.
2. For each group containing won cells, `load` and sum that group's counters across every holder's region for the pack (SeqCst); wait with backoff until the sum is 0.
3. **Timeout (`evict_wait`, 2 s):** if a group's sum has not reached 0, CAS the won cells of that group back `evicting -> present` and count `evict_timeouts_total`. Nothing has been journaled yet (wait first, journal second).
4. For cells whose group is clear: **append `PUNCH` records for the whole batch and fsync the journal once** (section 8), then `fallocate(PUNCH_HOLE | KEEP_SIZE)` over each contiguous run of won cells only, then CAS `evicting -> absent`.

Why this is correct: reader and evictor each store to one location then load the other's (the Dekker pattern); with SeqCst on both, either the reader sees `evicting` or the evictor sees the counter, never neither. Once a cell is `evicting` no new reader can start on it, so a sum of 0 observed in step 2 stays valid through the punch. The post-read recheck is a second, independent guard. T10a checks the protocol with an exhaustive interleaving model (loom) **including claim, publish and evict transitions on neighbouring cells and a reader that straddles groups**, so the proof is a test.

### 5.2 The FUSE library `tsfuse` (in this repository; Linux only)
**[D]** No vendored fuser. A crate `tsfuse` in this repository, designed for tierstore, serves a read-only tree and the put directory. The komira-ai/fuser fork is retired when `tsfuse` serves in production (T-R); **macOS does not block this (5.6).**

**Scope.** Linux only. Opcodes implemented:
- **Tree mount (read-only):** INIT, DESTROY, LOOKUP, FORGET and BATCH_FORGET (no-ops: inode numbers are record indexes in the mapped image), GETATTR, OPEN/OPENDIR/RELEASE/RELEASEDIR (5.3), READ, READDIR, READDIRPLUS, READLINK, STATFS, INTERRUPT. Everything else: `ENOSYS`.
- **Put directory mount:** the above plus CREATE, WRITE, FLUSH, FSYNC, SETATTR (size, times), MKDIR, UNLINK, RENAME. T-F9 fixes the list from the current put handler.
- Not implemented: xattrs, locks, bmap, ioctl, poll, notify store/delete/inval, passthrough, macOS/BSD.

**INIT flags to negotiate (fixed in T-F1).** Required: `ASYNC_READ` (without it the kernel serialises reads per file), `PARALLEL_DIROPS`, `MAX_PAGES` with an explicit `max_readahead`, `READDIRPLUS` and `READDIRPLUS_AUTO`, `CACHE_SYMLINKS`, `SPLICE_WRITE` (and `SPLICE_READ` for the put mount), `HAS_RESEND`, `NO_OPEN_SUPPORT`, `NO_OPENDIR_SUPPORT`. Never assumed: anything the kernel did not offer. INIT negotiates down to the kernel's minor.

**Shape**
1. **ABI module.** Hand-written `#[repr(C)]` structs for the opcodes above, parsed and encoded with `zerocopy`-style derives (size and alignment checked). The uapi header of the farm kernel is pinned in the repository; **T-F1 builds a farm probe that prints `sizeof` and `offsetof` of every struct** and a Rust test compares them.
2. **tokio-native dispatch with reply affinity.** Each reader owns a **cloned** `/dev/fuse` descriptor (`ioctl(FUSE_DEV_IOC_CLONE)`) driven by `AsyncFd`. [K] A reply is matched by `unique` against the **processing queue of the device it is written to**; clones share the connection and the pending queue but each has its own processing queue. **So a reply is written to the descriptor that read the request, not to any clone.** `Replier` carries its originating device (`Arc<DevFd>` for `/dev/fuse`; a handle to the owning queue's submit channel for io_uring, since a SINGLE_ISSUER ring cannot be submitted to from an arbitrary task) and completes by `write(2)` on that fd (or by sending to the ring task). `Replier` is `Send + 'static`; completion from any task is allowed, on the originating device.
   - The `unique -> cancel token` entry is **registered at read time**, before `Fs::handle` runs; `Replier::complete` is a single atomic take, so exactly one of the real reply and the `EINTR` reply is ever written. An INTERRUPT read by another clone between read and park finds the entry and is answered correctly.
   - A reader reads into a pooled buffer, parses in place, and calls `Fs::handle(req, replier)` returning `Handled::Done` or `Handled::Parked`. Metadata and ready reads complete inline with no `await`; a parked operation copies the few fields it needs and returns the buffer. A tree session sets a tiny `max_write` (8 KiB read buffers); the put session sizes to `max_write`.
   - **Clones do not remove the shared pending-queue lock** [K]: all clones poll one input queue, so a new request wakes every `AsyncFd` and N-1 readers see EAGAIN. Clones reduce contention on the processing queues only. The benchmark (V3 with N readers) is expected to show a knee, not linear scaling.
3. **Interrupts.** INTERRUPT cancels a parked operation through the token table and replies `EINTR`; an INTERRUPT that arrives before its request is answered `EAGAIN`. A SIGKILLed build frees its parked read.
4. **io_uring transport (kernel 6.14+).** Registers `FUSE_IO_URING_CMD_REGISTER` entries per queue and replies with `COMMIT_AND_FETCH`.
   - **`fuse.enable_uring` is set at boot** (kernel command line or module options in the hostkeeper node image), never at runtime. At startup `tsfuse` reads it; if 0 it uses `/dev/fuse`. Both transports share dispatch code behind a `Transport` trait.
   - **Hybrid, not alternative [K, uncertain]:** INIT, early requests, INTERRUPT and FORGET may still arrive on the `/dev/fuse` path in uring mode. The library keeps one `/dev/fuse` reader alive beside the rings and routes replies to those requests on it. T-F0 checks INTERRUPT delivery in uring mode and the INIT-then-register order; conformance item 4 runs on the uring transport.
   - **Parked reads hold ring entries.** A cold storm can occupy every entry of a queue and stall metadata behind it. Mitigations: depth per queue large enough for the expected parked count, small payload buffers for the tree session, Prepare making cold reads rare, `uring_entries_parked`. **The storm that must pass is `depth x queues x 1.5` parked reads**, and metadata p99 must not grow.
   - **Memory:** the kernel has one queue per CPU (88 here) and every queue registers entries each with a header and payload buffer: `queues x depth x (header + payload)`. At depth 64 and 256 KiB payload that is about 1.4 GiB per mount, over 10 GiB at 8 job mounts. **io_uring is the default for a mount only if the registered memory is at most 64 MiB at the depth that passes the storm test** (otherwise a shared node-wide mount, or `/dev/fuse`); T-F0 computes the per-mount cost.
   - **No splice on this transport:** one more copy than splice from the page cache; measured.
   - **Takeover is not available on io_uring until proven:** see item 5. A mount that must survive a process update uses `/dev/fuse`.
   - T0 E records `kernel.io_uring_disabled`, the seccomp profile of job sandboxes, memlock limits, and the FUSE request timeout (`fuse request_timeout`, 6.14+: a parked cold read can abort the whole connection if an administrator set it).
5. **HAS_RESEND takeover with every clone retained.** [K] `FUSE_NOTIFY_RESEND` requeues requests from the processing queues of devices that still exist; when a process dies, `fuse_dev_release` ends the in-flight requests of each clone it alone held with an error. **The supervisor (fd store) therefore holds the original descriptor and every cloned descriptor**; the new process receives all of them (SCM_RIGHTS), skips INIT, and sends RESEND; resent requests are answered under their original unique id on the descriptor that holds them. Conformance item 7 kills the server with N > 1 readers and several parked reads on different clones and asserts no EIO and exactly-once completion. io_uring: ring entries of a dead process are torn down and their requests failed unless the kernel has uring resend [K, uncertain]; the T-F2 spike answers; until then uring is takeover-incapable.
6. **Mounting.** Direct `mount(2)` with `fd=`, `rootmode`, `user_id`, `group_id` with `CAP_SYS_ADMIN`; otherwise `fusermount3`. Options: `ro` (tree), `nosuid`, `nodev`, `default_permissions`, `allow_other`.

### 5.3 Serving path
**Metadata from the mapped image.** The mount `mmap`s the tree-list image read-only (`PROT_READ`, `MAP_PRIVATE`; `MADV_RANDOM` for records). LOOKUP binary-searches the sorted directory records, GETATTR reads the node record, READDIR/READDIRPLUS encodes straight from records. Entry and attribute validity are long (the tree is immutable). The image file is immutable, never punched or truncated. **Page-in on a cold page of the image is an inline stall on a reader thread:** at tree open the mount calls `MADV_WILLNEED` on the record sections and, for a hot mount, `mlock`s the directory and node sections (bounded: the image is small relative to the pack data), so a first touch does not block on the shared cache SSD during heavy writeback. **File data is not mmapped.**

**Opens.** A tree session negotiates `NO_OPEN_SUPPORT`/`NO_OPENDIR_SUPPORT` and answers the first OPEN per connection with `ENOSYS` (that first handle costs one round trip; the kernel then stops sending OPEN). **T-F0 confirms** that the kernel keeps the page cache across opens in this mode, including for that first handle; if not, the library returns `FOPEN_KEEP_CACHE` on OPEN. The benchmark reports both regimes. The put session handles OPEN/CREATE normally.

**Reads (`/dev/fuse` transport).**
1. Resolve the node to its span(s), map to pack and cell range, run the 5.1 reader protocol. If every cell is `present`: reply from the pack's data fd.
2. **Reply by splice:** `splice(data_fd -> pipe)` then `splice(pipe -> /dev/fuse)` with `SPLICE_WRITE`. **The out header length is set from the bytes actually spliced:** loop until the full length is in the pipe; only a read at EOF may be short; a short splice or a hole read with a wrong `oh.len` makes the kernel reject the write with EINVAL and leave the request unanswered. **Pipes are sized with `F_SETPIPE_SZ` to at least `max_read` plus the header** (the default 64 KiB pipe fails a 256 KiB splice). Small replies (starting threshold 16 KiB) use `pread` into a pooled buffer and `writev`. [K, uncertain] `FUSE_SPLICE_MOVE` normally fails on page-cache pages and falls back to a copy, so splice saves one copy, not all; V4 measures it.
3. **Double page caching.** After a span is served the second copy is dropped only by the server on `durable` cells (4.8). **All three choices (splice versus pread, KEEP_CACHE/NO_OPEN, DONTNEED policy) are measured.**
4. Not ready: `Handled::Parked`; completion later on the originating device (5.2). A fill that is lost (4.6) completes every parked read of the tree with EIO.
- **io_uring transport:** same logic; the payload is copied into the ring entry's buffer and committed.

D-state is reduced, not eliminated; `fuse_d_state_threads` stays an acceptance metric.

### 5.4 Mount to tierstore protocol and runtimes
- One long-lived multiplexed connection per mount, request ids, out-of-order completion, **the server spawns a task per request**.
- Messages: `open-pack(tree_ref, pack_idx, holder) -> (data fd, state memfd, region)`; `wait(pack, off, len) -> ok | eio(cell) | lost`; `ensure(pack, cell_runs, class, rank)`; `release(holder)`. The handshake sends the counter memfd and the lifeline socket once (5.1).
- **Epoch.** The server stamps every connection and reply with a random u64 epoch; the mount drops replies from a previous epoch. On reconnect the mount re-handshakes with the same holder id (counter memfd and lifeline are unchanged), re-sends `open-pack`, and re-issues every parked `wait` idempotently. Backoff 50 ms doubling to 2 s with jitter.
- **Coalescing of waits.** A `wait` covers the remaining span up to the next 8 cells; concurrent reads join the pending `wait`.
- **Runtimes are separate.** `tsfuse` readers run on **dedicated threads** (one current-thread runtime or plain thread per reader), because ready reads run `pread`, splice or an image page fault inline and may block; the protocol client runs on its **own** runtime; they are joined by channels. A reader blocked in the kernel cannot stall the `wait` completions that unblock other reads. T10a has a test where every reader thread is blocked in a fake `pread` while `wait` completions still flow.

### 5.5 No global locks across I/O
`pinning` and `lifecycle` are removed; state is per pack. The pin fix of komira-ai/tierstore#343 stays as a bridge until this lands.

### 5.6 macOS: same engine, same cache, NFS front
macOS is not a blocker for anything in this document.
- **Front.** On macOS tierstore-fs is the NFSv3 server (the komira-ai/nfsserve fork) running **inside the tierstore process**. It does not use fuser, so retiring the fuser fork (T-R) is not blocked by macOS. `tsfuse` and the FUSE conformance suite are Linux-only.
- **Read path.** The NFS `read`, `lookup`, `getattr` and `readdir` handlers call the same engine (`ensure`/`wait`), the same cell cache and per-cell verification, the same L2 reader (the mapped list image) and the same storage-tier client (10.6) as the Linux mount. A Mac has no storage drives; it is a pure client of the storage tier's read path. The NFS handler is async, so a not-ready read awaits readiness the same way a parked FUSE read does.
- **What differs, behind an `EntryStore` trait.** (a) The NFS server is in-process, so the state page needs no memfd, no SCM_RIGHTS and no counter memfd: an entry is an in-process object with atomic cell states and an in-process reader count per group; the same CAS rules and the same evictor ordering apply (the Dekker argument holds within one process; the loom model covers both implementations via the shared trait). (b) Hole punching uses `fcntl(F_PUNCHHOLE)` on APFS [K, T-M1 confirms on the Macs]; if a volume cannot punch, eviction falls back to whole-entry eviction only. (c) Durability uses `F_FULLFSYNC`. (d) No splice, no io_uring, no `O_DIRECT`: reads are `pread`, with `F_NOCACHE` where a measurement says it helps. (e) The journal, `recheck` and boot-id rules of section 8 are shared code.
- **L2 and macOS.** Macs run the same tierstore binary, so the reader-first rollout (3.5) covers them; the CAS service's dual emission keeps old Mac binaries working until updated.
- **Tests.** The `EntryStore` conformance suite runs against both implementations on a Linux node (in-process) and on the Macs (T-M1, T-M2). Mac builds stay release-only; these tests are not per-PR.

## 6. Prefetch
Mount provider `Prepare` at job assignment runs on the same engine.
1. Read the tree list through the cache; mmap PACKS and CELLS.
2. Planner (a pure function of list, ranges table and profile; T7): per pack, the needed cell set, ordered by the profile's first-touch order else tree order. Output `Vec<Ensure>` with ranks.
3. **Gap rule in cells (per drive):** a part reads one file on one drive, so reading through a gap costs gap bytes at the media rate against one positioning. Merge needed runs across a gap of up to `gap_cells = ceil(pos_ms x media_MBps / 1.048576 / 1000)` absent cells: about 3 at 12 ms and 190 MB/s for a cold HDD, and 0 for a hot NVMe copy. T0-F sets `pos_ms` and `media_MBps`. Gap cells that are fetched are real cells: verified, written and `present`, not discarded.
4. **Whole-pack rule, budget-aware.** `cov` = needed fraction of the pack's cells after merging: `cov >= 50%` fetch the whole pack; pack <= 64 MiB and `cov >= 20%` fetch the whole pack; otherwise merged runs only. If the node's queued backlog exceeds 2 s of budget throughput, a would-be-whole pack is downgraded to runs. `amplification` (bytes fetched / needed) must stay under 1.5 in T10b or the constants move. A later demand on missing cells of a `Partial` entry fills gaps; it never starts a second entry.
5. Prepare is cancellable: a released holder demotes its unstarted parts to `background`; they are dropped only if no other holder wants them.
6. Prepare never gates launch.
7. Prepare takes a hold on every pack of the tree, and that hold lasts until the job's mount session is released (one hold per tree per job), so packs the job has not opened yet stay held in the node cache (7).

This replaces the S3 hydrate queue, the FUSE `prefetch all` threads and the Prepare groups.

## 7. Eviction, holds and admission (the node cache)
- **Accounting by cells.** Use = (`present` + `claimed` + `recheck`) cells x cell size, charged at claim time and refunded on failure. The critical-mark reserve check applies at claim time per cell run.
- **Eviction order.** (1) Whole unheld entries by LRU. (2) If only held entries remain, **cell-group eviction** of held entries by the 5.1 protocol (candidate cells in 16-cell groups untouched longest by the touch epoch; CAS per cell), counted in `punched_bytes_total`.
- **Admission at job assignment.** The jobscheduler asks `reserve(estimated_held_bytes)`. **`held` = the planner's needed cells minus cells already `present`.** Refuse when `held_cells_charged + estimate > 0.9 x budget`; refusal reasons `budget`, `enospc`, `oversize` are metered. A pack larger than the budget is refused only if its needed set exceeds `budget - held`.
- **Holds** are refcounts per pack: mount sessions (via `open-pack`, released by lifeline hangup or `release`), Prepare, and the fill while parts are in flight. One lease per session. Holds are local to the node cache: the storage tier never evicts for capacity (10.5), so it needs no `touch`; a held pack is lost only to a DELETE or to loss beyond the redundancy (4.6).
- **Startup.** No eviction and no scrub until a 60 s grace window since process start, so mounts rebind their holds. The data file is `flock`ed by its owner; a process that cannot lock an entry reads and evicts nothing from it. A directory lock `packs-v2/.lock` makes exactly one process the evictor.
- Poisoning is per cell (4.6). The storage tier has no capacity eviction; its hot tier reclaims only clean copies (10.5).

## 8. Crash safety and journal
- **Order of a cell's durability:** verify in memory -> `pwrite` -> state `present` (servable now) -> later, in a batch: `fdatasync(data file)` -> set the `durable` bit -> append a journal record `(ADD, run start, count, crc)`. A journal record never precedes durable data.
- **Punches are journaled before they happen, in batches:** after the evictor has observed every counter sum at 0 (5.1), it appends a **batch** of `PUNCH` records (run start, count) and fsyncs the journal **once**, then punches. A record is durable before its punch. Replay applies ADD and PUNCH in order, so after a crash a punched cell is never resurrected as `present`. A crash between the record and the punch only leaks blocks that a later refetch overwrites.
- **Batching.** One committer per node commits every 1 s or at 32 MiB pending, with one `fdatasync` per dirty entry file; never one commit per part or per demand sub-range.
- **Snapshot.** The journal is folded into `<hash>.meta` (present bitmap, pack_root, size, last use, **`boot_id`**) at entry close, at eviction and every 64 records, by write-temp, fsync, rename and directory fsync.
- **Restart.** Load the snapshot, replay the journal; only committed cells are restored. The snapshot and journal carry `/proc/sys/kernel/random/boot_id`. **Same boot and a process crash** (SIGKILL, oomd kill, panic): the kernel and the page cache survived, journaled cells follow durable data, so they are restored `present` with no recheck. **Different boot, or no boot_id (power loss, kernel crash, unclean marker):** every restored cell is `recheck`: the mount cannot serve it; the server re-hashes it on first demand or by a low-priority sweep and flips it to `present` or `absent`. This covers devices that reorder flushes; the cost is bounded by the cells actually read.
- `fdatasync` cost through the loop device is measured in T0 B and is one reason for section 11. The entry path includes `pack_root`, so a changed pack never reuses a file.

## 9. S3-front large objects
- Objects over `whole_fill_limit` (1 MiB on the node) that miss in the S3 front are routed to the pack engine with `PackId = (key, ETag)` and `digest=none`; for a stored object the ETag is its content-derived generation (10.2).
- **No stale mix.** For a stored object the generation is known from `LOCATE` before the first part (10.6), so every part carries `If-Match` from the start. For an object with no known generation (silo-origin through the legacy upstream), the first part is issued **alone**; its response gives the ETag, and every later part carries `If-Match`. When the S3-front index already holds the ETag, no part starts without it.
- **Integrity.** For a `digest=none` object the storage tier keeps a crc32c in every record header and a **per-cell crc32c table per block** (in the block trailer and the shard footer, 10.2, 10.4); the serving node verifies the crc of every whole cell it serves for such an object (hardware crc32c, negligible CPU) and returns an error on mismatch, which the client treats as a verification failure (4.6: the other hot replica, or degraded read of the cold stripe). The tables are in the node's memory, so no extra seek happens; packs with cell digests are verified by the client instead. A bad copy is quarantined and repaired (10.3, 10.4), never rewritten in place.
- Ranged S3 GETs are served from `present` cells of a `Partial` entry and wait on missing cells at `demand` class. A write-through PUT's generation is the hash of its bytes, so it names the same `PackId` the storage tier will report. Keys are create-only (10.9); a different object needs a different key. Objects up to `whole_fill_limit` stay in the node's `obj/`. T12 owns routing.


## 10. The storage tier: hot NVMe and cold HDD on three storage nodes (replaces silo)

Where each part of the owner's brief is answered: (a) roles 10.1; (b) hot tier 10.3; (c) cold tier 10.4; (d) movement 10.5; (e) configuration 10.14, including the hot-only mode; (f) S3 semantics 10.9; (g) metadata, ordering and recovery 10.2; (h) capacity 10.15; (i) why not SeaweedFS, Ceph or silo 10.12; (j) migration 10.10 and 10.11. Units are defined in 10.2. Reading from build nodes is 10.6, the drive scheduler 10.7, membership and audit 10.8, risks 10.13.

### 10.1 Roles and scope [D]
**Storage node** (3): runs the tierstore daemon with `role = "storage"`. It owns the node's NVMe and fifteen HDDs and runs, in one process: the S3 front (the store's front door), the peer service (10.6), the hot store (10.3), the cold store with one `DriveSched` per HDD (10.4, 10.7), the key index and block table (10.2), and the background workers (destage, promote, re-replication, rebuild, scrub, GC; 10.4, 10.5). It runs no builds, no cache and no FUSE mount.

**Build node** (31): `role = "build"`. It runs the node cache, `tsfuse` or the NFS front, the fetch engine and an S3 front whose upstream is the storage cluster. **A build node holds no durable data**: its cache can be lost and refilled; nothing the store promises depends on it. It reaches the storage cluster over the private network, never any drive directly.

**Combined node** (`role = "combined"`, the NOW profile of section 21): one process runs both roles on one machine: the storage roles on its own drives and a partition of its SSD, and the build roles (cache, mount, fetch engine) beside them. The protocols are the same; a part whose bytes are on the node itself is read locally without a network hop. Its cache stays a cache: durability is still only the storage roles'.

**The CAS service** PUTs packs and lists to the S3 front of any storage node (an endpoint list, config); the front routes to the object's writer node (10.2). Build nodes read through the engine (sections 4 and 6), which asks the storage nodes for a location and then fetches parts straight from the node that holds the bytes (10.6).

**One daemon, one config schema, no external database, no consensus service.** The three storage nodes do not elect a leader or run a metadata service. Every ownership decision is a pure function of the key and the membership document (10.2, 10.8); every durable fact is in a replicated append-only log, a sealed block or a block-table record (10.2); the few decisions that two nodes could make differently (the sealed length of a segment, a stripe's descriptor, a stripe's free) are **majority-committed records** on the three nodes' NVMe (10.2), and every other race is settled by a cluster stamp or a create-only rename. The operator, not a protocol, decides that a node is `out`.

**Scope.** The store is a store kind (`store.kind = "tiered"`) for the farm's pack store with the S3 subset of 10.9: create-only PUT, range GET, HEAD, DELETE and prefix LIST. A proxy with a hot tier in front of an S3-compatible cold store (configurations A and C of tierstore-design.md) keeps its `s3:` or `dir:` cold store, unchanged; its configuration check refuses `tiered` as a cold store by name.

### 10.2 Units, data model, ordering, index and recovery [D]
**Units.** Each mechanism has one unit, and the units are not mixed:

| Unit | Size | It is the unit of | It is never the unit of |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Object** (a pack, a list, a small object) or a **byte range** of it | any | read, fetch, LOCATE, `If-Match`, promotion, delete | placement or erasure coding |
| **Record** | one object of at most `hot.large_min` (4 MiB), plus a 64-byte header | append, replication batch, index entry | a read beyond its own range |
| **Block** | at most `cold.block_bytes` (1 GiB) | storage, sealing, placement, erasure coding, destage, rebuild, scrub, GC | read, fetch or promotion |
| **Cell** (client) | 1 MiB of pack bytes | verification on the build node (section 3) | anything in the store |

A cold read reads exactly the object's byte range from the one drive that holds it (the offset comes from the index); a degraded read decodes only that range from `k` shards (10.4); a promotion copies only the object (10.5). **A block moves whole only on destage, rebuild or a reclaim rewrite, never on a read.**

**Object, key, generation.** An object is a key and a byte sequence. Its **generation** is the 128-bit blake3 of its bytes, computed while they stream in; it is the ETag, the `If-Match` value of 4.6 and the identity of "the same bytes". A PUT of a key whose index entry has the same generation is **identical** (200); a different generation is a **conflict** (412) (10.5). The optional PUT header `x-ts-expiry: <class>` (a small integer chosen by the CAS service, for example the week an object is expected to die) names an **expiry class**; it only groups objects physically (10.5) and never deletes anything.

**Replica set and writer.** For a key `K` the storage nodes are ranked by rendezvous hashing: `score(n) = H(K || node_id)` (blake3 reduced to u64; equal weights), `rank(K)` = nodes by descending score. The key's **replica set** at PUT time is the first `hot.replicas` **live** nodes of the ranking whose hot device is `in` (10.8), taken as an unordered set, and its **writer** appends and replicates while the other members mirror. **The writer rule:** when the replica set is every live storage node (our cluster: `hot.replicas = 3` on three nodes), the writer is the first live node of `rank(K)`, so the three nodes share the writes evenly and each writes its own open segments; when the set is smaller than the cluster, the writer is one member chosen by a fixed rule of the set that makes every node writer of the same number of sets (with three nodes and pairs, each node writes exactly one pair; T-S1 defines it). With three nodes every probe is at most three nodes, so there is no probe-depth rule: any lookup can ask all of them (10.6). The key's **index nodes** are its replica set at PUT time: with `hot.replicas = 3`, every storage node indexes every key.

**Cluster stamp [D].** Every storage node keeps a **hybrid logical clock**: a u64 of 48 bits of wall-clock milliseconds and 16 bits of counter, advanced on every local event and merged (`max`, then increment) with the stamp carried on every peer message it receives. A stamp is therefore comparable across nodes, and a stamp taken after a node has heard of an event is larger than that event's stamp whatever the wall clocks say (skew only moves stamps away from wall time, never reorders causally related events). Records, relocates and tombstones carry the stamp of the node that wrote them, and **the newest fact about a key wins**:
- A record's stamp is assigned by the writer at append. A **tombstone**'s stamp is at least one more than the stamp of every record of the key that the deleting writer found; a **re-PUT** of a key that has a tombstone takes a stamp above it. Both writers first ask every live storage node for the key (one parallel round trip, overlapped with receiving the body; the answers carry the node's clock and any record or tombstone of the key) and answer 503 when the answer is not definitive (the rule of 10.6 step 3).
- A **relocate** (GC rewrite, pack assembly) carries the stamp of the record it moves; at equal stamps a relocate supersedes the record, and it applies only while the entry still holds that stamp.
- **Liveness rule:** a record `(K, generation, stamp s)` is live iff no tombstone of `K` has a stamp greater than `s`. Because the rule is per key and not per `(K, generation)`, a delete followed by a re-PUT of identical bytes (same generation) is correct: the old record is older than the tombstone and stays dead, the new one is newer and is live.
- The two cases this fixes, as tests (section 14): a PUT on writer A, then a DELETE while A is down served by B, then an index rebuilt from trailers and the tombstone log: the tombstone's stamp exceeds the record's because B asked for the record before stamping, so the key stays deleted; and a re-PUT of the same bytes after a DELETE is live after a rebuild, not dropped as "older than the tombstone".

**Record.** A record is: header of 64 bytes (magic, flags, key length, data size, generation, crc32c of the data, **stamp**, crc32c of the header), key bytes, data, padding to 16 bytes. Records are self-describing, so a segment can be scanned without any index.

**Block.** A block is a sequence of records followed by a **trailer**, `block_bytes` long at most (default 1 GiB). Two kinds: a **segment block** (a sealed hot segment: small-object records in arrival order) and a **pack block** (assembled at destage from large objects, each record 4 KiB-aligned; 10.5). The trailer lists every record of the block sorted by key (key, generation, offset, length, crc32c, stamp), then a per-cell crc32c table (one per 1 MiB cell of the block's bytes), then a footer (entry count, offsets, the block's blake3). The trailer is a deterministic function of the block's bytes, so two copies of the same bytes have the same trailer. A block is immutable once sealed. **No object straddles two blocks, except a chain:** an object larger than `block_bytes - cold.block_reserve` (1 MiB reserved for its header, alignment and a one-entry trailer) is stored as consecutive blocks each holding one slice of that object and nothing else (an object of 2.5 GiB is three blocks); a range read touches only the blocks the range covers. An object of at most that size always fits in one block alone, so "just under 1 GiB" never needs a chain.

**Ids.** `block_id` = (allocating node id: 8 bits, per-node counter: 56 bits); `stripe_id` likewise. Nothing needs a central allocator.

**The index [D].** Each storage node keeps an **ordered map** `key -> entry` for every key whose replica set at PUT time included that node, plus a tombstone entry for every key whose tombstone it holds (every node holds every tombstone, below). An entry is: generation (16 B), block id (8), offset (4), length (4; a chain entry stores the total size and the first block), stamp (8), flags, and, after a promotion, the promoted copy's location (10.5). **It is kept in an embedded ordered key-value store on the NVMe (`fjall`, an LSM-tree crate under a permissive licence), not a hand-rolled in-memory map with snapshots.** Reasons: at 1e8 keys an in-memory `BTreeMap` costs about 180 to 220 bytes per key [I] (12 to 20 GB per node) and a consistent snapshot of a mutating map every few minutes is either a copy-on-write structure, a stop-the-world copy or a log mark we would have to build; the embedded store gives ordered range scans for LIST, point reads for `LOCATE` from its block cache, and continuous persistence. **The store is a cache of the bytes, not the source of truth:** its own durability is not relied on (it runs with its journal flushed only at the replay mark below), and every fact in it is rebuildable from segments, trailers, `blk/*.idx` files, shard footers and the tombstone log. T-S3 measures bytes per key on disk and in cache, point-read latency and bulk-load time at 1.4 M and 1e8 keys; `redb` (a copy-on-write B-tree, same licence class) is the alternative behind the same `Index` trait if `fjall` fails those measurements. A key is indexed on **`hot.replicas` nodes** (all three in our cluster), so any one node loss, or two, leaves every key indexed on a live node. **Objects do not move when they are destaged**: an object's offset in a sealed hot segment is its offset in the cold block, so destage changes the block's state, not the index (10.5). Only a GC rewrite or the assembly of a pack block from large files repoints an entry (a **relocate**, 10.5). Each node also keeps, per block, the count and bytes of live entries it indexes (`live_entries[block]`, `live_bytes[block]`), which the free protocol of 10.5 reads.

**Block table.** `block_id -> {state, length, hot copies [(node, file)], stripe_id, shard index}`; and `stripe_id -> {k, m or replicas, block ids and lengths, shards [(node, drive, shard file)], expiry class, coordinator}` (the **stripe descriptor**, a few hundred bytes). Every storage node holds the whole table, in the same embedded store and as small files on its NVMe (a bound per mode [E]: at `4+4` about 245 TiB / 8 GiB, 31 k stripes, and at `4+2` about 41 k; at `cold.replicas = 2` one record per block, about 125 k; a few hundred thousand small records at most).

**Majority-committed records [D].** Three kinds of block-table record decide something two nodes could otherwise decide differently: the **seal** of a segment (its sealed length, 10.3), a **stripe descriptor** (10.5) and a **stripe free** (10.5). Each is written to every live storage node's NVMe and is committed when `floor(nodes / 2) + 1` (2 of 3) have it durable. A seal additionally uses a ballot (10.3) so that two concurrent proposals cannot both commit. A single-node install commits on its own NVMe.

**Tombstones [D].** DELETE appends a tombstone `(key, generation, stamp, origin node, origin seq, names)` to the writer's **tombstone log** (`tomb/`); `origin seq` is a per-origin sequence number, so each log is totally ordered. Tombstones are tiny, so every log is **replicated to every storage node**, not only to the key's index nodes: a DELETE is acknowledged when a majority of the three nodes has the tombstone durable (and every index node that answered the key check, whichever set is larger), and the origin keeps pushing until every node not `out` has it. Each node keeps **one cursor per origin** and pulls `tomb?origin=<n>&after=<seq>` from each peer whenever it has missed a push or lost contact with that peer (not only after being `down`), and at startup, staying **`indexing`** until the pull is done; so no node answers for a key deleted while it was away or slow. **Applying a tombstone** is idempotent per `(key, stamp)`: a node that holds an entry of the key turns it into a tombstone entry and decrements the live counts only on that live-to-tombstone transition; every node keeps the tombstone entry whether or not it indexed the key. A tombstone applied late is also checked against the sealed trailers and `blk/*.idx` files the node already holds and extended to name any block that lists an older record of the key. Blocks are immutable, so the trailer of a block still lists a deleted object; the liveness rule above is what keeps it deleted when an index is rebuilt. **What a tombstone names** is every block or large file that may list a record of the key with a lower stamp: the block or large-file name of the current entry and every block the entry was relocated from that has not been freed yet (an entry keeps that list, `prior_blocks`, until each is freed). At a seal and at a descriptor commit, each trailer entry is checked against the tombstone entries, and a tombstone of a key in the new block is extended to name that block, which covers a large file deleted while its pack block was being assembled. **One drop rule:** a tombstone is dropped only when everything it names has been freed on every storage node that is not `out`: a stripe free committed and applied, a hot block or large file unlinked on every copy, or a committed hot-only block-drop (10.14), which counts as the free of that block. Each node reports when it has applied such a free; the reports are a replicated `tomb-drop(origin, seq)` record, pulled like a tombstone, and a node deletes the tombstone only when every node not `out` has reported. Then no trailer, `blk/*.idx`, copy or large file anywhere still lists an older record of the key. A LIST never shows a tombstone entry.

**Recovery after a restart (g).** No object is looked up in an external database, and an index is always rebuildable from bytes the node or its peers hold:
1. **Replay mark.** Every `index.flush_interval` (default 1 minute) a node flushes the embedded store and records, in the same flush, the **replay mark**: per open segment the committed length the index reflects, and the tombstone log position.
2. **Replay.** For each open segment, scan records from the mark to the last record whose crc verifies; for each segment sealed since, read its trailer; for large files, `readdir` of `big/` (the file name carries key hash, generation and size, so no file is opened); then replay the tombstone log tail. The record scan of one open segment is at most a few hundred MB of NVMe reads.
3. **No usable store (damaged or new NVMe).** Rebuild from the trailers: hot blocks from the node's NVMe; cold blocks from the sorted index files `blk/<block_id>.idx` that every index node keeps on NVMe (written at seal, replicated with the block), and if those are lost too, from the trailer at the end of the data shard of each cold block (one read per block, parallel across the 45 drives, minutes [E]); then replay the tombstone log; for each key keep the newest fact by stamp (the liveness rule). The rebuild is a bulk load in key order. Peers can also stream their index range for the keys this node should hold (`index-sync`).
4. Until a node's index is built it answers **`indexing`** (never a miss, 10.6).
Every index node applies the same records, relocates and tombstones with the same stamps, so the index nodes of a key converge on the same entry, and an index entry is as durable as the record it points to.

### 10.3 The hot tier on NVMe [D]
**Purpose.** Absorb every write at NVMe speed, serve recent and re-read data fast, and hold data until it is sealed and destaged. It is **replicated only** (`hot.replicas = 3` with `min_acks = 2` in our cluster, decision Q2; there is no erasure coding on the hot tier: it is small, and the point is speed). It is deliberately small against the cold tier (10.15).

**Layout** (one xfs filesystem on the node's NVMe, `noatime`):
```
/mnt/nvme/ts-hot/
  NODE                     node id, cluster id, format version
  seg/<block_id>.seg       a WRITE segment: open, or sealed (dirty until destaged, then clean); a replica's copy has the same name and the same bytes
  seg/<block_id>.idx       sorted trailer index of a sealed segment
  big/<hh>/<khash>.<gen>.<size>.obj    a large object, whole file (key hash, generation, size in the name)
  big/.<khash>.<nonce>.up              a large upload in progress
  cache/<boot>.<seq>.seg   a CACHE segment: promoted copies only (clean, single copy, never destaged, 10.5)
  tomb/<seq>.log           tombstone log (replicated)
  blk/<block_id>.idx       index file of every block this node indexes, hot or cold (kept after destage)
  index/                   the embedded ordered store (index, block table, replay mark)
  stripes/<stripe_id>.desc, seals/<block_id>.seal
```
**Write segments and cache segments are never mixed** [D]: `seg/` and `big/` hold new data, which must be destaged; `cache/` holds copies of objects whose durable copy is cold, which may be dropped at any time. The destager reads only `seg/` and `big/`, so a promoted copy can never be destaged twice.

**Small objects: Haystack-style append-only segments.** An object of at most `hot.large_min` (default 4 MiB) is appended as a record to the **open segment of its writer, replica set and expiry class**: there is no size class and the replica set is unordered, so on three nodes there are three open segments per expiry class in the cluster (with `hot.replicas = 3` one per writer node, each replicated to the other two; with pairs one per pair), at most 24 with `gc.classes_max = 8`, and 3 today (every object is class 0 until the CAS service sends the hint). Records are written with buffered `write` at 16-byte alignment (a 1 KiB object costs about 1.1 KiB, not a 4 KiB block) and made durable with `fdatasync`. A new segment file is preallocated to `hot.seal_bytes` if T0-S finds that an `fdatasync` that extends a file costs more than one into preallocated space on the U.2 device (it measures appended, `fallocate`d and pre-written files); the default is whichever is cheaper.
- **Group commit.** One committer per open segment, leader-follower with no timer: while a batch is being made durable, arriving records queue; when it finishes, the next batch starts at once. A batch is at most `hot.batch_max` (4 MiB). The latency of a small PUT is one local `fdatasync` in parallel with one replica round trip, not a timer.
- **The index entry becomes visible after the ack rule is met** (below), under the key lock; a second PUT of the same key waits on the first's outcome and then compares generations (identical or conflict).
- **Power-loss protection is a stop condition [D].** The group-commit latency and the ack rule assume that an `fdatasync` on the U.2 NVMe costs tens of microseconds, which needs power-loss protection. If T0-S finds the device without PLP, the defaults of `hot.min_acks` and `batch_max` are not just retuned: the owner is asked before T-S4 freezes them, because every small PUT then waits for a flash program on two nodes.

**Replication protocol (the segment is the unit).**
1. The writer appends the batch locally and streams the **same bytes at the same offsets** to every other member of the segment's replica set: a message carries `(segment id, ballot, start offset, bytes, running crc32c of the segment through this batch, the writer's stamp)`.
2. A replica appends at that offset, `fdatasync`s and acknowledges with its committed length. It rejects a batch whose start offset is not its committed length, whose running crc does not match, or whose ballot is lower than the highest it has promised for that segment (below).
3. **Ack rule [D].** A PUT is acknowledged to the client when the record is durable on **`hot.min_acks` copies** (default = `hot.replicas`; the writer's own `fdatasync` counts as one). Every acknowledged record is therefore on every copy of the default configuration. `min_acks` below `replicas` is allowed and documented as weaker (a record acknowledged on fewer copies is lost if the node that held it alone is lost before the segment is re-replicated).
4. **Seal by majority [D].** A segment's sealed length is decided once, by a ballot over the three storage nodes (a single-decree agreement: the same majority as the descriptor commit of 10.2, not a service). A **ballot** is `(round, node id)`, ordered lexicographically; a segment opens at round 1 under its writer.
   - *Prepare.* The sealer (the writer for a size, age or early seal; a replica taking over from a writer that stopped answering) picks a ballot above any it has seen for the segment and sends `seal-prepare(segment, ballot)` to all three storage nodes (nodes without a copy vote too; they hold block tables). A node that has promised no higher ballot promises this one durably and returns any seal proposal it has accepted, and, if it holds a copy, its committed length and running crc. A copy that has promised a ballot rejects batches under any lower one, which fences the old writer.
   - *Choose.* With promises from a majority: if any node returned an accepted proposal, the sealer re-proposes the one with the highest ballot (**a committed seal is never changed**); otherwise, among the `q` copies that answered (its own included), it proposes the **`r`-th longest** crc-verified length, `r = max(1, q - replicas + min_acks)`. At the default `min_acks = replicas` that is the shortest: every copy holds every acknowledged record, so none is cut, and every answering copy already holds the proposed bytes, so the seal never depends on a tail that only one copy has. Below the default, a record acknowledged on `min_acks` copies is kept whenever at least `r` of them answered; a copy shorter than the proposal fetches the difference in Converge. A copy reports both its committed length and its **verified** length (the crc-verified prefix); a copy whose verified length is below its committed length has damage inside acknowledged bytes and counts as not answering for Choose (it is repaired from a peer by the scrub path). The proposal is never below the sealer's own last acknowledged offset; if the rule would put it there, the sealer does not propose and the segment waits (writes go to a new segment). The writer's last acknowledged offset is at most this length, so an early seal by the writer and a take-over by a replica choose by the same rule.
   - *Accept.* `seal-accept(segment, ballot, length, crc)`; the seal is **committed** when a majority has accepted it durably. Then `sealed` is broadcast.
   - *Converge.* Each copy makes its bytes equal to `[0, length)`: it truncates an unacknowledged tail beyond the length, or (a copy that did not answer and is shorter) fetches the missing bytes from an answering copy and verifies the running crc. It appends the trailer (a deterministic function of the bytes) and checks the block's blake3 with the sealer's.
   - *Index.* Records within the sealed length that a copy had not indexed (written durably on every answering copy but not acknowledged, for example because the client's ack was lost) become visible; that is the same object a client retry would write, so it is allowed, and `hot_unacked_records_adopted_total` counts it. Records beyond the length were never acknowledged and never indexed.
   **Two sealers under a partition** (the writer A seals early at its last acknowledged offset while the replica B, cut off from A, seals at its longer committed length) cannot both commit: two majorities of three intersect, and the second to prepare learns the first's accepted proposal. Destage, re-replication and trailer distribution act only on a committed seal. With one node alone on its side of a partition, it cannot seal; its open segment stays open and its writes fail the ack rule (503), which is the documented behaviour of a minority.
5. **Replica loss: seal and roll.** If a replica stops acknowledging for `hot.replica_stall` (default 5 s [I]), or the membership says it is `down` or `out`, the writer **seals the open segment early** (step 4, which raises the ballot) and opens a new segment with a replica set of live nodes, so writes continue at full replication at once, without copying a prefix first. The early-sealed block is under-replicated until **re-replication** (a `background` copy of its file to another live node, 10.7; part of milestone S1, 18) restores `hot.replicas` copies; `hot_under_replicated_blocks` counts it. With fewer than `hot.min_acks` live nodes, small PUTs answer 503 `Retry-After`.
6. **Seal triggers [D].** A segment is sealed when it reaches `hot.seal_bytes` (default = `cold.block_bytes - cold.block_reserve`; a record that does not fit seals the segment and opens the next), or by an early seal above, or by age: past `hot.seal_max_age` (24 h) **only if it holds at least `hot.seal_min_bytes`** (256 MiB), otherwise when it is older than `destage.max_age` (3 days). So a slow class does not produce a stream of small blocks (at today's 34 GB/day a pair fills 1 GiB in about 2 to 3 hours; a class at 1/8 of that reaches the floor in about 5 hours [E]). A sealed segment is never written again; the block's state moves to `sealed` (the file name does not change).

**Large objects: whole files.** An object over `hot.large_min` is a file `big/<hh>/<khash>.<gen>.<size>.obj`. The writer streams the body into `.up` on its NVMe (`fallocate` when the length is known, 4 MiB writes) and, at the same time, to each other member of the replica set, which writes its own `.up`; the writer computes the generation while streaming and sends it in a trailer that every node checks against its own hash (a mismatch unlinks the `.up` and counts as an abandoned copy). Each node commits with `fsync`, a create-only check by the index under the key lock, `renameat2(RENAME_NOREPLACE)` and a directory `fsync`. The PUT is acknowledged on `hot.min_acks` committed copies. A replica that lags for `hot.put_copy_stall` (10 s [I]; time in the drive queue counts, a client pause does not) is abandoned and the writer re-sends the finished local file to another live node, so no giant buffer is held; if too few copies remain the PUT answers 503 and the `.up` files are removed when the writer's connection closes. A crash leaves only `.up` files, removed by the startup sweep. A large file has no seal: it is immutable from its rename.

**Reads on the hot tier.** A record is read with `pread` at `(offset, length)` from the node's copy (the page cache is advised away on sealed segments after a read, `fadvise(DONTNEED)`, so the NVMe's pages do not crowd out the node's job; T0 B decides O_DIRECT); a large object with `sendfile` or `pread` ranges. Any copy can serve; the client picks one by advertised queue wait (10.6). Every record read verifies the record crc32c (a failure serves another copy and queues repair of that segment).

**Hot restart.** Section 10.2 recovery; a replica that was down comes back, pulls every peer's tombstone log (and block-drop and `tomb-drop` records) from its per-origin cursors, staying `indexing` until it has applied them, asks the seal records of the segments it mirrors, adopts any committed seal (truncating or catching up its copy) and catches up the open segments it still mirrors from their writers.

**Hot scrub.** Sealed segments, large objects and cache segments are re-read in the background once per `hot.scrub_period` (7 days, NVMe is fast), verifying every record crc and the block's blake3; a bad copy is repaired from another copy (or, once destaged, from the cold tier), never rewritten in place; a bad cache segment is dropped.

**The NVMe is the node's single failure unit for hot data [D].** One NVMe per storage node holds that node's hot copies, its index and its block table. Losing it is, for hot data, losing the node: its hot copies are re-replicated from the other copies (10.8), its index is rebuilt (10.2 step 3) and its block table is copied from a peer. With `hot.replicas = 3`, dirty data survives the loss of one NVMe plus a loss on a second node once its third copy has landed; before that (the replication lag of one batch) it is on two devices (10.13, Q2).

**Failure domain.** `hot.failure_domain` is `node` or `drive` (a single-node install with several NVMe devices uses `drive`); replicas are placed on distinct domains. Configuration validation refuses `replicas` greater than the number of domains.

### 10.4 The cold tier on HDD [D]
**What it holds.** Only **sealed, immutable blocks** (10.2): sealed small-object segments and pack blocks assembled from large objects. Nothing small and nothing random is ever written to a cold drive: every write is a whole shard file of up to 1 GiB, written sequentially; every delete is the `unlink` of a whole shard file. This is the f4 / Windows Azure Storage shape (seal, then erasure-code lazily), chosen because it makes both the read and the write cost one sequential stream per drive and suits CMR and SMR drives alike (the drive type is a T0 fact).

**Redundancy is a configuration of the cold tier** (10.14): either `ec = { k, m }` with `block_bytes`, or `replicas = N`, with a failure domain `node` or `drive`.

**Stripe and shard (EC).** A **stripe** is `k` sealed data blocks of one expiry class plus `m` parity blocks. Block `i` of the stripe is **data shard `i`**; the shard file of a data shard **is the block's bytes, byte for byte**, so an object at `(block, offset)` is at file offset `offset` of one data shard. A stripe whose data blocks differ in length is encoded as if the shorter ones were extended with zeros up to the longest (parity length = the longest block rounded up to 4 KiB); the zeros are never stored. Reed-Solomon over GF(2^16) (`reed-solomon-simd`, MDS: any `k` of the `k+m` shards rebuild the rest), encoded in windows of 1 to 4 MiB per shard streamed from the `k` sources, so memory is `(k+m) x window`. The crate's decoder restores **data** shards only [V, checked before T-S8]: a missing parity shard is rebuilt by restoring the data windows (or reading them, when all data shards survive) and re-encoding, with no extra reads.

**Contiguous layout and "one read touches one disk".** In the healthy read path a read of an object of at most `block_bytes` is one sequential read of one data shard on one drive: records never straddle shards (they lie inside a block), and parity is not read. A block holds many records, so one drive's stream serves a whole pack. The only objects that cross a shard boundary are those larger than `block_bytes`, which are chains (10.2) laid out at block boundaries, so a range read still touches only the shards of the blocks it covers (a sequential read of a 3 GiB pack streams three shards, usually on three drives, in order). Striping is deliberately not used (HDFS-7285 stripes at 64 KiB to 1 MiB to make small files efficient and to parallelize large ones; here small objects are already packed by the hot tier, and contiguous blocks win on read cost and rebuild simplicity). The price is that one block is one spindle's bandwidth (about 190 MB/s [I]); promote-on-read (10.5) is the answer for a block that is hot.

**On-disk layout** (xfs on each HDD, `noatime,nodiratime,inode64`; keep xfs: extent allocation, extent-size hints, `fallocate`, cheap unlink of large files):
```
/mnt/hdd07/ts-cold/
  DRIVE                                     drive_id (UUID), node id, cluster id, format version, filesystem UUID
  s/<hh>/<stripe_id>.<idx>.shard            one shard of a stripe (idx 0..k+m-1); replicas mode: <block_id>.copy
  s/<hh>/.<stripe_id>.<idx>.<nonce>.up      a shard being written
  FAILED                                    present once the drive is failed (10.7); an operator removes it
```
- **Shard file:** data region at offset 0 (a data shard: the block's bytes including its trailer; a parity shard: parity bytes), padded to 4 KiB, then a **footer** of at least 4 KiB: format version, stripe id, shard index, `k`, `m`, the block ids and lengths of the `k` data blocks, the placement of every shard of the stripe at write time, a crc32c per 1 MiB cell of this shard, the blake3 of this shard, crc of the footer. A stripe descriptor (10.2) can be rebuilt from any one footer of a stripe never converted, and from any parity footer of a converted one (10.5).
- **Writes are sequential and whole-file [D].** `fallocate` the exact length, `O_DIRECT` writes of 4 MiB at depth 2 from one write stream per drive (10.7), then footer, `fsync`, `renameat2(RENAME_NOREPLACE)` from the `.up` name, directory `fsync`. A crash leaves only a `.up` file, removed by the startup sweep. A 1 GiB `fallocate` with a 64 MiB extent-size hint keeps the file contiguous (T0-F C-5 measures extents per file).
- **Space accounting:** fill is the filesystem's used bytes over its capacity (`statfs`), so bytes that are not the tier's (silo's, on the NOW profile of section 21, where the tier shares silo's drives) count against `fill_limit`; the tier's own share, the sum of allocated bytes of its shard files (`st_blocks`), is reported beside it. The marks (10.14): `cold.fill_limit` 85% (no new shard is placed on a drive above it) and a rebuild reserve (10.15).

**Placement: failure-domain aware, on 3 nodes x 15 drives.**
- Inputs: the `k+m` shards of a stripe, the node domains (3), the drives of each node (15), `max_shards_per_domain` (default `ceil((k+m) / domains)`).
- **Nodes:** the nodes are ordered by `H(stripe_id || node_id)`; shard `i` goes to node `order[i mod 3]`. With 4+4 that is 3 + 3 + 2 shards, with 4+2 it is 2 + 2 + 2; which node holds the lighter count rotates with the stripe id, and which shard indexes are parity rotates the same way (RAID-5 style), so data reads and fill spread over the nodes.
- **Drives within a node:** the node's `in` drives below `fill_limit` that hold no other shard of this stripe, by weighted rendezvous score `-free_bytes / ln(H(stripe_id || drive_id) / 2^64)`; the best-scoring drives take the shards. Weights are free bytes, so fuller drives are chosen less.
- **Invariants a placement must satisfy** (checked at write time and by a background **placement audit**, which repairs a violation by moving a shard like a rebuild does): the `k+m` shards are on distinct drives; no node holds more than `max_shards_per_domain`; and, **for the node domain, `m` is at least `max_shards_per_domain`** (so the loss of any node leaves at least `k` shards). A configuration that fails the last rule cannot survive its own declared failure domain and is **refused at startup** (a 10+4 code on 3 nodes puts 5, 5 and 4 shards on the nodes and fails the rule; that is SeaweedFS's fixed code, 10.12). A single-node install sets `failure_domain = "drive"` and the rule is `m >= 1`.
- The placement is **recorded** in the stripe descriptor and in every shard footer; it is never recomputed, so a membership change moves nothing by itself.

**Choice of code for 3 nodes** (n = k+m shards of a stripe on distinct drives; overhead = n/k; usable = 229.5 TB raw at the 85% fill limit divided by the overhead [E]):

| Scheme | Shards per node | Overhead | Logical capacity | Survives a node loss | Node loss plus one more drive | Reads to rebuild one shard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `replicas = 2` | 1 copy on 2 of 3 nodes | 2.0x | 114.8 TB | yes | no (the second node's copy is the only one) | 1 |
| `replicas = 3` | 1 per node | 3.0x | 76.5 TB | yes | yes | 1 |
| `4+2` | 2 / 2 / 2 | 1.5x | 153.0 TB | yes, exactly (m = 2 = shards per node) | no | 4 |
| `6+3` | 3 / 3 / 3 | 1.5x | 153.0 TB | yes, exactly | no | 6 |
| **`4+4` (this cluster)** | 3 / 3 / 2 | 2.0x | 114.8 TB | yes, with one spare shard | **yes** (m = 4 >= 3 + 1) | 4 |
| `5+4` | 3 / 3 / 3 | 1.8x | 127.5 TB | yes, with one spare | yes | 5 |
| `10+4` (SeaweedFS OSS fixed) | 5 / 5 / 4 | 1.4x | 163.9 TB | **no** (a node holds 5 > 4) | no | 10 |
| LRC (12,2,2), two-level codes | needs about 16 domains | 1.33x | 172.1 TB | **no** | no | 6 (local) |

- **No code is cheaper than 1.5x if a node must be survivable on 3 nodes** [E]: losing a node loses `s` shards, so `m >= s >= n/3` and `n/k >= 1.5`. This holds for every code (LRC and product codes only cut repair reads), so the real choice is 1.5x (a node loss uses all parity) against about 2x (a node loss plus a drive).
- **Recommended default for this cluster: `4+4`** (Q1; the tradeoff is stated in the decision table). Reasons: (1) after a node loss, `4+4` still has one spare shard, and **it can be rebuilt onto the two survivors at 4 + 4 shards, each node holding `m = 4`, so it survives the next node loss too**; `4+2` would have to put 3 shards on a survivor, which exceeds its `m`, so a lost node stays unrecoverable to full tolerance until a replacement node arrives. (2) Today under 2% of the cold capacity is in use (1.34 TB live of 115 TB logical), so the second spare costs idle disk and no capacity anyone needs; the code is recorded per stripe, so once logical use passes half of the guarded capacity (about 36 TB at `4+4`, 10.15) the operator switches the default to `4+2` for new stripes and a background re-encode (read `k`, write fresh parity, same shard layout) can convert old ones. (3) Repair reads are 4 shards, the same as `4+2`. (4) A node reboot, which hostkeeper rolls through one at a time, leaves `4+2` at zero parity for its duration and `4+4` at one. **For other installs** the schema default is `4+2` on 3 or more domains with `failure_domain = node`, and `replicas = 2` or `3` on 2 or 3 domains when the operator prefers no parity math.
- **Not offered by the schema as a recommendation:** codes wider than the domain count allows, EC with overwrite (every cold write is a whole new stripe), striped layout.

**Replication variant (`cold.replicas = N`).** There are no stripes: each sealed block is destaged alone as `N` whole copies `<block_id>.copy` on distinct domains by the same node and drive placement rule; a read uses one copy, a repair is a copy, `4+4`-style parity math is absent. Everything else (sealing, index, GC at block granularity, the scheduler) is shared.

**Degraded reads [D].** A read of a data shard is degraded when its drive is `stalled`, `failed`, `suspect` with a slow queue, or answers an error or a crc mismatch, or its node is `down`.
1. The node that owns the shard (if the node is up and only the drive is bad), or else the **reconstructor** (among the live nodes holding shards of the stripe, the one whose `k` shortest advertised drive queues are shortest, named by LOCATE, 10.6), serves the request. The requested range is rounded to 4 KiB.
2. It reads the same range from `k` other shards of the stripe (live data siblings first, parity as needed; a sibling shorter than the range contributes zeros), preferring shards on its own node and on drives with the shortest advertised queue, and decodes the missing range.
3. It verifies the result against the record's crc32c (small objects) or leaves cell digests to the client (packs, 3.3); on a mismatch it re-reads whole cells and checks each survivor's cell crc32c from the footers, excluding a corrupt survivor.
4. Cost: `k` reads of the range from `k` drives, latency bounded by the slowest of them, **admitted as `k` stream charges** on those drives' schedulers and charged `k` parts by the client (4.3); **hedging** (4.6) starts a degraded read in parallel when the data shard's drive is slower than its baseline for a `demand` read of at most 4 MiB. The whole shard is **not** rebuilt on a read; that is the rebuilder's job. A degraded read of a block queues its stripe for repair (10.8) if one of its shards is missing or bad.
- The degraded fraction is small and under our control: a single drive failure degrades 1/45 of the cold reads while its stripes rebuild; a node down (a planned reboot or an outage) degrades the reads of the data shards on that node, about 1/3 of cold reads, for the outage [E]. **Load with one node down:** each degraded read costs `k = 4` range reads, so the total drive-read volume is 2.0 times the healthy volume, carried by 30 of the 45 drives (3.0 times the healthy load per surviving drive), and the cold read capacity falls from about 8.5 GB/s to about 2.8 GB/s [E] (4.3); 17.2 item 5 measures it against a pass bar. No source publishes f4's or Backblaze's degraded-read share, so none is quoted.

**Rebuild of a lost drive (declustered, no hot spare) [D].** A drive is **lost** when it is `failed` for `rebuild.drive_delay` (default 10 minutes; a drive that returns clears it) or removed by the operator.
- Every stripe with a shard on it is queued, **fewest surviving shards first** (a stripe at exactly `k` survivors first). For each, the rebuilder reads `k` surviving shards **in full and sequentially** (a `background` stream on each source drive), decodes the missing shard in windows, and writes it as one sequential shard file to a drive chosen by the placement rule (same node preferred, never a drive of the stripe, never above `fill_limit`); then it commits the descriptor change (`stripe_id`, shard index -> new drive) and unlinks nothing (the lost drive is gone). The stripe's margin is restored as each shard commits.
- **Spare space is spread, not reserved on a drive:** the capacity guard keeps one node's raw plus one drive per remaining node free (10.5, 10.15), so a rebuild always has somewhere to write. Rebuild writes may exceed `cold.fill_limit` on a drive, up to `cold.rebuild_fill_ceiling` (92%), while any stripe is below its tolerance: weighted placement can push single drives past the limit when the free space is uneven, and a rebuild must not stop for that. New stripes from destage never exceed `fill_limit`.
- **Time [E, inputs: 190 MB/s per drive, 25 Gbit/s links, 1 GiB shards; every shard rebuilt costs `k = 4` shard reads and one shard write].** At the capacity guard (the most a drive holds unless the operator disables it) a drive holds about 142.8 TB / 45 = 3.2 TB, about 2,950 shards: reads 4 x 3.2 = 12.7 TB and writes 3.2 TB, spread over 44 drives, are about 0.36 TB per drive, **about 32 minutes at full spindle speed and about 2.1 hours at the default 25% share** (`rebuild.share`). A drive at `fill_limit` with the guard off (5.1 TB) is about 0.58 TB per surviving drive, about 51 minutes, and **3.4 hours at 25%**. The network carries the reads that cross nodes (about three quarters of them [I]), about 9.5 to 15 TB, 0.9 to 1.4 hours on one 25 Gbit/s link. At today's fill (under 2%) it is minutes.

**Rebuild of a lost node [D].** A node is **lost** when the operator marks it `out` (a held step) or, by default, after `rebuild.node_down_after` (default 24 hours) of being `down`; a reboot or an outage shorter than that rebuilds nothing, so a rolling update never starts a rebuild storm.
- Hot blocks: re-replicated to the surviving nodes (a file copy per block, 10.3).
- Cold shards: every stripe that had shards on the lost node is rebuilt as above onto the two survivors, **subject to the same placement invariant** (`<= m` shards per node, so 4 + 4 for `4+4`; a `4+2` stripe cannot be restored beyond four shards on two nodes, and the audit reports `cold_stripes_below_tolerance`). With a replacement node, the placement audit later moves shards back to restore 3 domains.
- **Time [E].** One decode per stripe restores every shard the stripe lost on the node (2 or 3 at `4+4`; the decoder restores data shards and parity is re-encoded from them, so no extra reads), so reads are `k` shards per affected stripe, not `k` per lost shard. At the capacity guard the cluster holds at most 142.8 TB raw, about a third of it on the lost node: **47.6 TB to write**, and reads of 4 of the 8 shards of every stripe, **71.4 TB**. Over the 30 surviving drives that is about 4 TB per drive: **about 5.8 hours at full spindle speed and about 1 day at a 25% share**. The network moves at most the 119 TB of reads and writes between the two survivors, about 5.3 hours over two 25 Gbit/s links, so the drives at their share are the bound. Afterwards the survivors hold 142.8 TB of 180 TB raw (79%), below `fill_limit`, with one drive's worth of free space each for the next drive failure. At today's fill it is minutes.
- **Margin 0 while a node is down [D].** With a node down, `4+4` stripes that had 3 shards on it have one spare; a drive failure in that window leaves them at margin 0, and a margin-0 rebuild reads exactly the four survivors, so one latent sector error loses the stripe. Therefore, while any node is `down`: (1) each surviving drive's scrubber first verifies the shards of stripes at margin 1 or less (chosen from the descriptors, no extra I/O), so a latent error is found and repaired while the spare still exists; (2) a stripe at margin 0 is rebuilt at `prepare` class ahead of every other repair; (3) `cold_stripe_margin{margin="0"}` above zero raises an alert (the operations document carries the rule). `rebuild.node_down_after` (24 hours) still decides when the node's own shards are rebuilt.

**Scrubbing [D].** Each drive's scrubber, a `background` stream with its own bounded rate, re-reads every shard file sequentially once per `cold.scrub_period` (default 30 days; a full 6 TB drive is 8.7 hours of reading), verifying every cell crc32c against the footer and the shard blake3. A mismatch quarantines that shard (`.bad`), counts `cold_scrub_bad_total`, and queues the stripe for repair: the shard is **recomputed from the other shards and written to another drive** (never repaired in place). A weekly sample (`cold.deep_scrub_fraction`, default 1% of stripes per week) additionally decodes a parity-consistency check: it reads all `k+m` shards of the stripe and verifies that re-encoding the data shards gives the stored parity (it catches a parity shard whose crc matches but whose bytes are wrong, for instance a defective encoder).

### 10.5 Write path and movement: PUT, destage, reclaim, promote, delete and GC [D]
**Four movements, four rules.** **Destage** copies dirty data (sealed write segments and large files) to the cold tier, soon after sealing, as sequential writes; it never deletes anything. **Reclaim** (eviction of the hot tier) only drops clean bytes, by one unlink or one index-entry removal; it never copies and never waits on a hard drive. **Promotion** copies one object from cold into a cache segment; the cold block stays the durable copy. **GC** frees dead blocks whole and rewrites a block only when it is mostly dead. Un-destaged data is never reclaimable; if destage lags, writers get backpressure, and reads never stall on it.

**PUT, front to back.**
1. The CAS service sends one streaming `PUT /<bucket>/<key>` (Content-Length if known, else chunked; `x-ts-expiry` optional) to the S3 front of any storage node. The front computes `rank(K)`, the replica set and its writer (10.2), and forwards the stream to the writer (a front that is the writer keeps it).
2. **Size decides the path:** Content-Length at most `hot.large_min` (4 MiB) is a record in an open segment; larger is a whole file (10.3). A chunked PUT is buffered up to `hot.large_min + 1` bytes first, so a body that ends inside the buffer takes the small path.
3. While the body arrives, the writer asks every live storage node for the key (10.2: the answers give the create-only verdict and merge the cluster stamp). It runs the 10.3 protocol and acknowledges when the record or file is durable on `hot.min_acks` copies. **Outcomes:** `200` with `ETag` = generation when committed or **identical** (same key, same generation: an idempotent retry or a race of two writers of one object converges); `412` when the key holds a different generation (the PUT leaves nothing behind); `503` with `Retry-After` when fewer than `hot.min_acks` copies could be made, when the key's answer is not definitive (a node `indexing`, or `hot.replicas` nodes down), or when hot dirty bytes are above `hot.dirty_slow` (below); `507` when hot fill is above `hot.dirty_reject` after reclaim, or no cold placement exists for a stripe and hot is full.
4. **What create-only guarantees [D].** When the storage nodes' membership views agree, create-only is linearizable: one writer per key, the key lock, the all-node check of step 3. When views differ (a node is `down` in one front's view and not in another's, for the few minutes of 10.8), two fronts can route one key to two writers; with content-derived keys both carry the same bytes and converge as identical. Different bytes under one key in that window are **detected, not prevented**: `LOCATE` then returns two generations, `store_key_conflict_total` counts it, the later stamp's entry wins on the index nodes, and no reader mixes bytes (every GET of a fill carries the fill's pinned generation, 4.6).
5. **Create-only after a delete:** a deleted key is absent; a later PUT creates it with a stamp above the tombstone's (10.2). The CAS service's keys are derived from content, so a PUT of different bytes under a used key is a client defect, answered 412 while the key exists.

**Hot dirty bytes** are the bytes of open and sealed write segments and of large files whose objects are not yet committed to the cold tier. They are the only hot bytes that must not be lost, and the only ones that count against backpressure (clean bytes are reclaimable, below).

**Destage: seal, then copy to cold (automatic, decoupled from reclaim) [D].**
- **Eligible:** a block whose seal is committed (10.3), or large files, older than `destage.min_age` (default 1 hour). The destager reads `seg/` and `big/` only, never `cache/`.
- **When it runs:** whenever hot dirty bytes pass `hot.dirty_destage_start` (default 50% of the NVMe) it destages down to `hot.dirty_destage_stop` (35%); and independently any eligible bytes older than `destage.max_age` (default 3 days) are destaged, so the NVMe is a bounded-age window and the cold tier is the durable base. The work is a `background` stream rate-limited by `destage.bytes_per_hour`, raised to above `prepare` at `hot.dirty_urgent`. With `destage.min_age = 0` and `destage.max_age = 0` (a single-node install, 10.14) it destages as soon as a block seals.
- **Which first:** within an expiry class, oldest sealed first (least recently read first among equals). Stripes are formed from blocks of **one expiry class** (10.2) so that blocks that die together are frozen together (GC below).
- **Stripe formation.** A stripe needs `k` blocks of one expiry class, **from any replica set**: the coordinator streams blocks it does not hold from a peer's NVMe. Among eligible blocks it groups those of **similar length** (sorted by length, consecutive `k`), so parity, which is as long as the longest block, wastes little on short ones. Until `k` eligible blocks of a class exist the blocks wait on the hot tier; after `destage.partial_after` (default 7 days) or when hot dirty bytes pass `hot.dirty_urgent`, a stripe is made from `j < k` blocks, the missing data shards being virtual zeros (10.4). **A partial stripe costs `(j + m) / j` raw per logical byte** (`4+4` with one block is 5x; with two, 3x; with three, 2.33x), so it is a pressure valve, not a default; `cold_partial_stripes_total` counts them and GC compacts them later (below). With `replicas = N` there are no stripes and each block destages alone. **`cold_fill_efficiency`** (live data bytes over raw cold bytes used) shows the combined effect of short blocks, partial stripes and dead bytes; T0-F C-10 replays it.
- **Pack assembly.** Large objects are assembled into **pack blocks** inside the destage pipeline: the coordinator takes a window of the oldest `destage.pack_window` (64) eligible large files of one class, places them **first-fit decreasing** into blocks of at most `block_bytes - block_reserve` (so a 600 MiB and a 400 MiB pack share a block instead of each leaving 400 to 600 MiB of a block unused; T0-F C-6's pack size distribution checks the waste before `block_bytes` is frozen), 4 KiB-aligns each record, and **streams each block straight into its data shard and the encoder**, writing the trailer at the end; the NVMe files are the copies until the stripe commits, so nothing is written twice on NVMe. An object above `block_bytes - block_reserve` becomes a chain (10.2).
- **Procedure.** (1) choose the blocks and the coordinator (the node that holds most of them locally; the others stream from a peer's NVMe); (2) allocate `stripe_id`, compute the placement (10.4); (3) open one writer per shard (local, or `PUT /store/v1/cold/<drive>/shard` to the peer service with a generation trailer); (4) stream each block from NVMe once, teeing the bytes to its data shard and the encoder, and the parity windows to the parity writers, all as write streams, 4 MiB sequential, one write stream per drive; (5) every writer checks the crc32c table and blake3 of what it received against the coordinator's, `fsync`s, writes the footer, renames; (6) **the descriptor commit is the atomic point**: the coordinator writes the stripe descriptor (naming itself as the stripe's coordinator) as a majority-committed record (10.2); (7) on commit, each node flips the block states to `cold`, applies the relocate for pack blocks (the block's `.idx` is sent with the commit; an index entry still at the same generation and stamp moves from its file to `(block, offset)`; a key deleted or replaced meanwhile is skipped), and marks the hot copies **clean**.
- **Failure.** A writer that fails or stalls is replaced by another drive of the same node (the shard is re-sent from NVMe). If a node cannot be reached the stripe is placed on the other nodes if the invariant still holds (`4+4` places 4 + 4 on two nodes; **`4+2` cannot, so destage of `4+2` pauses during a node outage and the hot tier absorbs**); otherwise the stripe is abandoned (`.up` files unlinked) and retried later. A stripe whose shards are all durable but whose descriptor never committed is an orphan, removed by a sweep after `destage.orphan_age` (1 day). Nothing is ever acknowledged to a client from a cold write: the hot copies stay until commit.
- **Verification:** by default each writer's crc32c/blake3 comparison plus a read of the last cell of each shard (`destage.verify = "tail"`); `destage.verify = "full"` re-reads every shard (cost: the stripe's bytes once more); the scrubber (10.4) is the continuous check.
- **Converting replicated cold blocks to erasure coding** (milestone S1 to S2, or a change of `cold.ec`): a block destaged as `replicas = 2` copies becomes data shard `i` of a new stripe **in place**. The coordinator chooses the stripe's `k` blocks and the shard-to-node and shard-to-drive assignment **from the existing copy locations** (not from the stripe id's node order), so that the invariants of 10.4 hold (distinct drives, at most `max_shards_per_domain` per node); a block for which no such assignment exists is copied like a placement-audit move instead. For each chosen copy it creates the shard name as a **hard link** (`linkat`) to the copy file, so the bytes have both names; it reads the `k` data shards, writes only the `m` parity shards, and commits the descriptor. Only after the commit does it unlink the old `.copy` name and the block's other copy. A crash before the commit leaves both copies intact under their old names; the orphan sweep removes only shard-name links and parity files, never a `.copy` name, before a commit; after a committed conversion it unlinks any `.copy` name whose block belongs to the committed stripe, so a crash between the commit and the unlink leaks nothing. A converted data shard keeps its copy footer (it names the block, not the stripe); the parity shards' footers carry the full descriptor, so the descriptor is rebuildable from any parity footer, and the scrubber verifies a copy footer as such. No data block is re-copied unless the placement requires it.

**Reclaim of hot space (eviction) [D].** Reclaim drops only clean bytes and never copies:
1. **Cache segments** (`cache/`), whole, least recently read first: one unlink, plus removal of the promoted location from the index entries they hold.
2. **Clean write segments and large files** (destaged, cold copy committed), whole file, least recently read first: one unlink (`hot.keep_clean = true` by default keeps them as a read cache until space is needed; `false` deletes at commit).
3. Nothing else: dirty bytes are never dropped.
A sealed write segment that is mostly dead before destage (`live < hot.gc_live_below`, 0.5) is compacted on NVMe (its live records re-appended to the open segment with relocates), which is cheap there and keeps dead bytes off the cold tier.

**Headroom and backpressure.** Marks on dirty bytes as a fraction of the NVMe, in order: `hot.dirty_destage_start` 50% < `hot.dirty_urgent` 75% (destage priority rises above `prepare` and partial stripes are allowed) < `hot.dirty_slow` 85% (small and large PUTs answer 503 `Retry-After`, the CAS service backs off; `hot_put_throttled_total`) < `hot.dirty_reject` 90% (507). The reject mark also applies to the filesystem's total fill after reclaim: an xfs filled with 1 GiB files and small files risks allocation failures above about 90% [I]. The invariant the operator checks: **NVMe capacity x (reject - start) must hold the burst that arrives while destage is stalled**; for our hardware that is a 6.4 TB logical hot tier at `replicas = 3` (19.2 TB of NVMe), 2.6 TB logical between the start and reject marks, which is about 75 days of today's 34 GB/day and about 3.8 days at 20x today's rate [E]. Destage pauses (never fails PUTs by itself) when cold cannot place a stripe (nodes down, no drive below `fill_limit`, the capacity guard below); `destage_stalled_seconds` alarms.

**Capacity guard.** `cold.node_loss_reserve = true` (default for `failure_domain = node`): destage stops placing new stripes when the cold raw bytes in use would exceed `fill_limit` of the capacity of **all nodes but the largest, less `cold.reserve_drives` drives** (default: one drive per remaining node; 2 on our three nodes, 1 on the NOW profile of section 21): `0.85 x (2 x 90 TB - 2 x 6 TB)` = 142.8 TB raw, which is 71.4 TB logical at `4+4` and 95.2 TB at `4+2`. So a node loss can always be rebuilt onto the survivors (10.4) and, after that rebuild, each survivor still has a drive's worth of free space for the next drive failure. An operator who accepts the risk disables it.

**Promote on read: a cache copy, not a move [D].** A cold read is one stream from one drive, which is a fine cost for a cold object and a poor one for a hot one (a block is one spindle's 190 MB/s shared by 31 clients).
- **Policy.** Each storage node counts demand and prepare reads that it serves from the cold tier (not scrub, rebuild, GC or destage) in a count-min sketch per `promote.window` (1 hour). An object read **`promote.reads`** (default 2) times in the window, and no larger than `promote.max_object` (default 256 MiB), is promoted, **unless hot dirty bytes are above `hot.dirty_destage_start`**: under write pressure nothing is promoted, so a promoted copy is never the first thing reclaimed right after it was made. The build nodes' SSD caches absorb repeat reads by one node, so a cold object that is read twice by the storage tier is, in practice, wanted by several build nodes.
- **What is copied, and where.** The object's bytes (never its block) are copied by a `background` stream into a **cache segment** (`cache/`) on the NVMe of the node that served the cold read (the data shard's node: a local HDD-to-NVMe copy, no network), with **one copy**. The **cold block stays the authoritative durable copy and is never rewritten to remove a promoted object.** The promoting node sends `promoted(K, generation, stamp, location)` to the key's index nodes, which add a second location `hot_clean = (node, cache segment, offset)` to the entry **only while its generation and stamp still match** (a tombstone that raced the promotion wins, and the copy is never used; a relocate keeps the hint, since the bytes are the same). The location is a cache hint, not a stamped fact: an index rebuild drops it; a node that restarts unlinks its cache segments instead of re-registering them, a cache segment's name carries the node's boot epoch so it is never reused, the index nodes drop every hint of a node whose boot epoch changed, and a hot read verifies the record's key and generation. LOCATE (10.6) returns it first, and falls back to the cold location if that node or file is gone. Dropping the copy is removing that location (and, when the whole cache segment is dropped, one unlink).
- **Cost on a small hot tier.** Promoted bytes are capped at `hot.promo_share` (default 20% of the NVMe), are never dirty, and are reclaimed whole-segment, least recently read first, with no rewrite; `promote.bytes_per_hour` (default 100 GiB) bounds the copy traffic. Promoting an object, not a 1 GiB block, keeps a hot 1 MiB object from costing 1 GiB of NVMe. A promoted copy is not a second durable copy and nothing relies on it.
- Today's read rate (0.5 MB/s cluster-wide) leaves this idle; it is built because the owner's design needs a path for a hot cold block, and its effect is measured in T0-F C-3b and T-B5.

**Deletes and garbage collection [D].** About 38% of pack bytes are already dead, and 14,795 of about 85.9 k packs are fully dead, because nothing deletes yet; deletes today are rare (0.035%) but the CAS service's planned GC will make delete volume a policy output. The tier is built for both.
- **DELETE** (`204` even when absent): the writer asks every live node for the key, appends a tombstone with a stamp above every record it found (10.2) to its tombstone log (replicated to every live storage node and acknowledged by a majority, 10.2), the index entry becomes a tombstone entry, the block's live counts are decreased, and a large hot file is unlinked on every copy holding it (a copy that is down replays the tombstone log when it returns, and unlinks). Blocks are never modified. A non-definitive answer is 503, never a silent partial delete.
- **Free without writing: the stripe-free protocol.** A cold block is **dead** when no index node holds a live entry pointing into it. A stripe is freed when all its data blocks are dead, by unlinking its `k + m` shard files (no I/O of data). The steps:
  1. **Owner.** The stripe's coordinator (recorded in its descriptor) owns its free; if that node is `out`, the next node of `rank(stripe_id)` takes it.
  2. **Count.** The owner asks every storage node for `live_entries[block]` of each data block of the stripe. A free waits while any node is `down` or `indexing` (a free is never urgent: the capacity guard, not GC, protects writes).
  3. **Commit.** If every node answers 0 for every data block, the owner writes a **stripe free** as a majority-committed record (10.2). From then the stripe is dead in every node's block table; a `LOCATE` that still names it gets a 404 on the shard, which the engine treats as a lost location and re-asks (4.6).
  4. **Unlink.** Only after the commit, each node unlinks its shard files of the stripe and the `blk/*.idx` of its blocks, and reports the free as applied (`tomb-drop`, 10.2); a tombstone is deleted only when every node not `out` has reported every free it waits for.
  5. **Free wins over repair.** A rebuild or placement move that tries to commit a descriptor change for a stripe whose free is committed is refused, and it unlinks the shard it wrote; the sweep also removes shard files of freed stripes.
  6. **No revival.** A PUT of a key whose old record lies in a dead or dying block always writes a new hot record (with a newer stamp); no index entry is ever pointed back into a block once its live count reached 0, and a relocate never targets a block in a stripe being freed.
  A sealed hot block that is wholly dead is unlinked at once on each copy (its live counts are local to its replica set, which holds every copy).
- **Group by expiry class [D].** Open segments and pack assembly are per expiry class (10.2) and a stripe holds one class, so blocks that die together are frozen together and whole stripes become free. Until the CAS service sends the hint every object is class 0 and GC depends on the rewrite below. `gc.classes_max` (8) bounds the classes; extra values fall into class 0. Classes affect placement of bytes only, never retention.
- **Rewrite only when it pays.** A stripe with a live fraction below `gc.rewrite_below` (0.30) is **rewritten** only when the cold fill passes `gc.rewrite_fill` (60%): a `background` job reads the live records through the normal read path, re-appends them (same key, generation and stamp, same expiry class) into hot write segments with a relocate each, and when every live record has moved the old stripe is dead and freed by the protocol above. Rewrite cost is the live bytes: freeing a stripe at 30% live writes 0.43 bytes per byte freed [E], against about 2.3 bytes written per byte freed for a vacuum at a 30% garbage threshold that rewrites whole volumes (SeaweedFS's default, 10.12). Because capacity is under 2% used, rewriting is off in practice today and only whole dead stripes are freed; `gc.rewrite_bytes_per_hour` caps it and it never runs while destage is `urgent`.
- **Partial stripes** (made under pressure) are the first rewrite candidates.
- **Tombstones** are dropped by the one rule of 10.2 (every block they name freed or unlinked on every node not `out`); a tombstone log segment is compacted when all its tombstones are droppable.
- **What is not claimed:** that GC never writes. Segments of small objects are garbage-collected like pack blocks (whole stripes, or rewrite), and the first rewrite pass is not measured; T0-F C-10 replays the measured death distribution against the grouping policy.

### 10.6 Read path and the peer protocol
**Lookup for a read of `(key, range)`, once per fill (not per part):**
1. **`LOCATE`.** The engine asks one storage node, with a batched `POST /store/v1/locate`: with `hot.replicas` equal to the number of storage nodes (our cluster) every node indexes every key, so it asks the **least-loaded** live node (the lowest advertised queue wait from earlier responses and the ping every 2 s); otherwise it asks the first live node of `rank(K)` (computed from the membership document; the build node keeps three connections). The answer comes from that node's index and block table (memory or NVMe), **never a hard drive**, and is one of:
   - **hot**: the generation and size, and the hot copies `[(node, file or segment, offset, length)]` of the object (the replica nodes, plus a `hot_clean` promoted copy if any, listed first, 10.5);
   - **cold**: the generation and size, and for each block the object covers `(block id, data shard node, drive id, shard file, offset, length)`, plus a **reconstructor** node for a degraded read and the `k` source drives it would read, chosen by their advertised queues (10.4), and the stripe's current shard health;
   - **absent**, **`indexing`** (the node's index is not built yet), or **unreachable**.
2. **Which node serves the bytes.** The engine pins the generation, picks a hot copy by the lowest advertised queue wait (`x-ts-queue-wait-ms` from earlier responses and the ping every 2 s; `suspect` drives last), and sends parts by `GET /store/v1/hot/<file>` or `GET /store/v1/cold/<drive_id>/<shard>` with `Range`, `If-Match: <generation>`, `x-ts-class`, `x-ts-job` and `x-ts-rank`, **straight to the node that holds the bytes**; the part does not pass through the node that answered `LOCATE`. With `hot.replicas = 3` the node that answered holds a hot copy itself, so a hot read is served by that node from its own NVMe with no cross-node hop; another copy is used only for a hedge, a retry or when that node's queue is longer. A cold shard on a `stalled` or `failed` drive, a crc error, or a `down` node sends the part to the reconstructor (`GET /store/v1/degraded/<block_id>?range`), which serves it by the degraded read of 10.4.
3. **A miss is definitive only when** `N - hot.min_acks + 1` live storage nodes, none `indexing`, answered `absent`, where `N` is the number of storage nodes: an acknowledged object is durable and indexed on at least `min_acks` nodes, and any `N - min_acks + 1` nodes include one of them. Our cluster (`N = 3`, `min_acks = 2`): a hit at the first node asked is final, and a miss needs a second node's `absent`, so with one node down the other two decide. Anything less is **not a miss**: the S3 front answers 503 with `Retry-After` (never 404), the engine waits and retries (4.6), no negative entry is cached and nothing is repaired from it. While the legacy upstream exists (10.10) a fill may read silo on a non-definitive miss too, but only a definitive miss imports. So a pack whose index nodes are rebooting is delayed, never reported missing.
4. **Negative cache.** A definitive miss is cached for 5 s **only for client GET and HEAD through the S3 front**, so 100 clients asking for a missing key cause one query. A local PUT of the key clears it. The engine filling a pack that a tree list names does not consult it (the tree is evidence that the pack exists); it single-flights one `LOCATE` per key.
5. **Corruption.** A record or cell that fails its crc or the client's digest is served from the other hot copy, or by degraded read of the cold stripe; the serving node is told (`x-ts-report: bitrot`) and queues repair of that segment or shard (10.3, 10.4).

**The peer service (every storage node).** HTTP/1.1 keep-alive on the **private network address only** (no public listener; TLS is not enforced and is an operator's choice on their own network; requests carry a cluster token read from a file, never an environment variable). Routes: `POST /store/v1/locate`; `GET /store/v1/hot/...` and `GET /store/v1/cold/<drive_id>/...` with the headers above (response headers: `ETag` = generation, `x-ts-queue-wait-ms`, `x-ts-service-ms`, `x-ts-drive-queue`, `x-ts-drive-state`; a queue over its bound returns `503` with `Retry-After`, a `stalled` or `indexing` drive `503` with `x-ts-drive-state`, never 404); `GET /store/v1/degraded/<block_id>`; internal routes between storage nodes (every message carries the sender's cluster stamp, 10.2): `PUT /store/v1/hot/replica` (segment batches with ballot and running crc, large-file streams), `PUT /store/v1/cold/<drive_id>/shard` (a shard write with its generation trailer), `POST /store/v1/descriptor` (stripe descriptor and stripe free commits), `POST /store/v1/seal` (seal prepare and accept with a ballot, 10.3), `POST /store/v1/key` (the writer's all-node key check before a PUT or DELETE, returning record and tombstone stamps, 10.2), `POST /store/v1/index-sync`, `GET /store/v1/tomb?origin=<node>&after=<seq>` (tombstone, block-drop and `tomb-drop` log pull, one cursor per origin), `POST /store/v1/repair`, `GET /store/v1/ping` (10.7: per drive state, oldest in-flight call age, queue depths by class, streams, membership epoch). It uses the same part engine on the client side: the `Upstream` trait gets a `StoreUpstream` implementation, so single flight, claims, verification, the controller and hedging are unchanged; only the transport, the `LOCATE` step and the choice among copies are new.

**The S3 front** on each storage node and the peer service share the same index, block table and schedulers.

### 10.7 Per-drive I/O scheduler
Every HDD has a `DriveSched`, owned by the storage node that holds it and run on **dedicated blocking I/O threads per drive, `drive.streams x drive.depth` of them** (2 x 2 = 4 by default), never the tokio blocking pool, so the configured depth is real and a drive stall cannot starve the async runtime. Fifteen of them run on each storage node. The NVMe has a simpler `HotSched`: a bounded queue depth (`hot.queue_depth`, default 64 requests [I]), the class order below, a demand-first rule, and the same stall watchdog; it has no slices, streams or preemption, because NVMe seeks cost nothing. The rest of this section is the HDD scheduler; its first-byte and slice constants are for a 7.2k SAS drive and T0-F replaces them.

- **Streams.** A *stream* is one request's sequential run over one file (a part read, a shard write by destage or rebuild, a scrub, repair or promote read). The scheduler admits at most `drive.streams` streams at once (default **2**), of which at most `drive.write_streams` (default **1**) write. A stream issues `chunk` = 4 MiB `O_DIRECT` requests at depth `drive.depth` (2), the device's `max_sectors_kb`; the arm sees ascending addresses within a file. Whether a read stream copies to the socket from an `O_DIRECT` chunk or uses buffered `sendfile` with `DONTNEED` is a T0-F arm; the default is `O_DIRECT` (exact device request sizes, no page-cache pollution on a node whose SSD is the cache).
- **Classes and order.** Waiting requests sit in class queues; a free slot goes to `demand` > `write` (shard writes of destage and rebuild) > `prepare` > `background` (scrub, GC and promote reads, non-urgent rebuild reads); a rebuild of a stripe with no spare shard counts as `prepare`. Within a class: deficit round robin across job ids, then `(rank, arrival)`. Among equal candidates the scheduler prefers the stream whose file's first physical extent (read once with FIEMAP at open and cached) is next above the current head position, an elevator over at most a few waiting streams.
- **Writes are never starved [D].** While a write waits, at least one slice in every four on that drive goes to a write (`drive.write_min_share`, 25%), whatever reads wait. So a read-busy drive slows a destage or rebuild write, never stalls it, and the destage backpressure of 10.5 sees progress.
- **Slices.** An admitted stream keeps its slot for `slice_bytes` (default 32 MiB, about 180 ms at 190 MB/s, so a seek of about 12 ms costs under 7%); at the slice boundary it yields only if another stream of equal or higher class (or a write owed its share) waits.
- **Demand preemption.** A `demand` request that finds only lower-class streams admitted takes the next chunk boundary of the lowest-class stream (that stream is paused and resumes later, costing two seeks); preemptions are limited to `demand_preempt_max` per second per drive (default 4), so a demand storm cannot turn the drive into a seek machine; beyond that a demand waits for the slice boundary. A write's guaranteed share is not preempted.
- **Small lane.** A read of one small record (10.2) does not take a stream slot. On a drive with an idle I/O thread it runs at once on that thread; otherwise it runs at the next chunk boundary of any admitted stream, on that stream's thread, at most one small read per boundary, by class order; `drive_small_reads_total`.
- **Read-ahead and slot release (slow consumers) [D].** Each read stream has a buffer of two chunks (8 MiB): an I/O thread reads the next chunk only while the buffer has room, and the socket writer drains it. If the buffer is full and the socket has accepted no byte for `drive.slot_release_after` (default 1 s [I]), the stream gives up its slot: it leaves the admitted set and is **parked** at its current offset, keeping its buffered chunks, and the slot goes to the next waiter. Parked streams are a separate set per drive, bounded by `drive.parked_max` (16), and do not count toward `queue_max`, so stopped clients never make new requests see 503; beyond `parked_max` the oldest parked stream is ended with an error and its client retries. A parked stream whose socket drains rejoins the waiting queue of its class with its original arrival rank. Parked buffers per node are bounded by `cold.parked_buffer_max` (256 MiB); beyond it a parked stream drops its buffer and re-reads at readmission. `drive_slot_releases_total` counts. A client whose hash pool pauses receive (4.7) therefore holds buffered bytes, not the spindle.
- **Watchdog and liveness [D].** Every blocking call a drive's I/O threads make records its submit time (an injected clock in tests): chunk reads and writes, `fsync` of files and directories, `renameat2`, `open`, `fallocate`, FIEMAP, `unlink`, the startup `readdir`. A watchdog task checks every drive each second; any such call outstanding longer than `drive.stall_after` (default 10 s [I]; T0-F C-9 reads the kernel's SCSI command and error-handling timeouts to place it) puts the drive in **`stalled`**: every queued request fails at once with `503`, `x-ts-drive-state: stalled` and `Retry-After`; new requests are refused the same way; other admitted streams on the drive fail at their next chunk boundary; shard writes in flight are re-placed on another drive (10.5); `drive_stall_total` counts. A thread blocked in the kernel cannot be cancelled: it is counted in `drive_io_threads_stuck`, and the drive leaves `stalled` only when every stuck call has returned **and** a probe read of the `DRIVE` file's block completes within `stall_after`; it is then `up`. Three stalls within an hour make the drive `failed` (below).
- **Liveness is the scheduler's, not the process's.** `GET /store/v1/ping` is answered from the scheduler's state: per drive its state, the age of its oldest in-flight call, queue depths and streams. A peer treats a drive whose oldest in-flight call is older than `stall_after` as `stalled` even before the owner's watchdog flips it.
- **I/O errors.** An EIO on a read of one shard file quarantines that shard (renamed `.bad`, repair of its stripe queued, 10.4); the drive stays `up`. `drive.eio_limit` errors (3) on distinct files within 10 minutes, or three stalls within an hour, make the drive **`failed`**: the node writes `FAILED`, serves nothing from it, and an operator clears it (no automatic retry loop on a dying disk). A write EIO re-places that shard and counts toward the same limit.
- **Backpressure.** Each class queue has a bound (`drive.queue_max`, default 8 requests). A read beyond it gets `503 Retry-After`. A drive whose write queue is full is not a placement target: the stripe coordinator picks another drive of the node (10.4); a write never waits unboundedly for a full queue. The client treats a 503 as a decrease signal **for that drive** (4.3) and a reason to prefer the other replica or a degraded read. **No number of clients can drive a spindle past `s*` streams.**
- **Instrumentation.** `x-ts-queue-wait-ms`, `x-ts-service-ms` and `x-ts-drive-state` on every response; per drive: state, streams active, queue depth by class, wait and service histograms, slice yields, preemptions, slot releases, small reads, stalls, stuck threads, bytes, util (from `/proc/diskstats`).
- **Constants [I until T0-S]:** `streams` 2, `write_streams` 1, `depth` 2, `chunk` 4 MiB, `slice_bytes` 32 MiB, `demand_preempt_max` 4/s, `queue_max` 8, `write_min_share` 25%, `slot_release_after` 1 s, `stall_after` 10 s. T0-S sweeps streams {1, 2, 3}, chunk {1, 2, 4, 8} MiB, slice {8, 16, 32, 64, 128} MiB, depth {1, 2, 4}, and runs the mixed, slow-consumer and churn arms of section 16.

### 10.8 Membership, states and audit
**Membership document** (`membership.toml`: epoch; the **cluster settings that every node must agree on**, `hot.replicas`, `hot.min_acks` and the cold code for new stripes, which each node checks against its own configuration at startup and which take effect with the epoch; per storage node: id, address, failure domain, role, and its hot device `in`, `drain` or `out`; per drive: id, node, mount path, weight = capacity, `in`, `drain` or `out`), distributed by hostkeeper as a file (configuration is files and flags). Only an operator changes it. Nodes piggyback `x-ts-mem-epoch` on peer responses and re-read the file on a higher epoch; views may differ for a few minutes, which the any-node `LOCATE` fallback (10.6) makes harmless. Build nodes read it to find the storage nodes. Drive identity is checked at startup: the path's `DRIVE` file and filesystem UUID must match the document, or the drive is not opened (a wrong-mount guard).

**States.** `out` is the only cluster-wide state; the others are a viewing node's view, either reported by the owner or concluded by the viewer.

| State | Set by | Reads | Writes (shards, hot) and repair targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| `up` | default; owner after index, stall or probation ends | yes | below `cold.fill_limit` (hard drives); below the hot marks (NVMe) |
| `indexing` | owner, from start until its index or drive list is built | 503 `indexing`; never a miss | no |
| `stalled` | owner's watchdog, or a viewer from the ping's oldest in-flight age (10.7) | 503; the stripe is read degraded | no; shard writes in flight are re-placed |
| `suspect` | the viewer: 3 failed pings or error responses; `up` again after 3 successes | last in preference | no |
| `down` | the viewer: the node answered no ping for 3 intervals (6 s); applies to all its drives | not asked; degraded read | no |
| `failed` | owner: `FAILED` written (10.7); cleared only by an operator | not asked | no; rebuild after `rebuild.drive_delay` (10.4) |
| `drain` | the membership document | yes | no new placements; its hot blocks are sealed and destaged, its shards moved or re-encoded elsewhere (section 21.3) |
| `out` | the membership document | not a candidate | no; its shards are rebuilt (10.4) |

**What happens in each case.**
- **A node reboots or is down for a while.** Reads of its hot copies go to the other replica, reads of its cold data shards are degraded (10.4), new PUTs go to the other nodes (the replica set is the first `hot.replicas` live nodes), new stripes are placed on the live nodes if the invariant holds (10.5). Nothing is rebuilt until `rebuild.node_down_after`; the open segments it mirrored are seal-and-rolled (10.3).
- **A drive fails.** Its stripes read degraded and rebuild after `rebuild.drive_delay` (10.4); with `cold.replicas`, its blocks are re-copied from the other copy (milestone S1); nothing else changes.
- **An NVMe fails.** For hot data this is the loss of the node (10.3): the hot copies it held are re-replicated from the other copies, its index is rebuilt from its peers (`index-sync`) and the cold trailers, and its block table is copied from a peer; its hard drives keep serving cold reads once the index is built.
- **A drive or node is removed for good** (`out`): rebuild (10.4), re-replication of hot blocks (10.3).
- **Adding drives or nodes.** New stripes place on them by the weighted rule; existing shards do not move; a background **placement audit** (low rate, `background` class) moves shards only to repair an invariant violation (for example after a replacement node arrives following a node loss). A fourth storage node extends the rank list; `hot.replicas` and the code are config.
- **Corruption** is detected by the record or cell crc, the client's cell digest, or the scrubber; the copy or shard is quarantined and repaired from the other replica or by decode.

**Audit [D].** Redundancy is measured, not assumed.
- **Stripe margin** (no disk I/O): from the stripe descriptors and the current drive and node states, `cold_stripe_margin{margin}` counts stripes by `surviving shards - k` (`< 0` is **unreadable**, `0` has no spare, and so on) and `cold_stripes_below_tolerance` counts stripes that would not survive the loss of a node; `hot_blocks_by_copies{copies}` counts hot blocks and large objects by live copy count. These are the numbers the availability claims of 10.4 are read against.
- **Live-key audit.** `tierstore store audit --keys <file>` takes a random sample of live keys that the CAS service supplies from its sweep (T-K1a), runs `LOCATE` and a one-cell read for each, and reports the histogram including **unreadable keys** and keys present in the index with no readable copy, as a report and as `store_audit_live_unreadable_ratio`.
- **Placement audit** (above) and the scrubbers' counters complete the picture.
- **Alerts** (in the operations document): any stripe or hot block below its tolerance for longer than `rebuild.drive_delay`; `cold_stripe_margin{margin="0"}` above zero while a node is down (10.4); `store_audit_live_unreadable_ratio` above zero; `destage_stalled_seconds` above an hour.

### 10.9 The S3 API subset and how the CAS service uses it
The S3 front serves the operations the CAS service needs and the engine's ranged reads:

| S3 operation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `PUT` object (`If-None-Match: *` or not; both are create-only) | Streaming PUT as in 10.5. No multipart, no copy, no overwrite. `200` if absent or identical (`ETag` = generation), `412` if the key holds a different generation, `503` with `Retry-After` if too few copies could be made or the store is slowing writers, `507` if full. Optional header `x-ts-expiry` (10.2). |
| `GET` with `Range` | One byte range (cell-aligned reads are the fast path; any range works). Served by 10.6. Without a range, the whole object as a stream. `503` (never `404`) while the answer is not definitive. |
| `HEAD` | From the index of the first reachable node: size, `ETag` (generation), no disk access. |
| `DELETE` | As in 10.5; `204` even when absent. |
| **`LIST`** (`ListObjectsV2` and V1: `prefix`, `delimiter`, `max-keys`, `start-after` or `marker`, `continuation-token`) | **Implemented.** The front sends `list(prefix, after, limit)` to every live storage node; each answers from its ordered index (10.2) in key order for the keys it indexes; the front merges the sorted streams, drops duplicates (a key is indexed on `hot.replicas` nodes), applies the delimiter roll-up (after emitting a common prefix it asks each node to **seek past** that prefix, so a rolled-up prefix of a million keys costs one seek, not a scan) and returns up to `max-keys` (at most 1,000) with the last key as the continuation token. It asks `N - min_acks + 1` live nodes, none `indexing` (two in our cluster; every node indexes every key there, so a page is answered by two nodes and merged); with fewer reachable it answers 503. Consistency: a key whose PUT was acknowledged before the call began is listed (the PUT was acknowledged only after its index entries were published on the ack-rule copies); a key deleted before the call is not; concurrent changes may or may not be seen. The CAS service's janitor and adopt step use it. Tombstone entries are never listed. |
| Multipart, versioning, ACLs, tagging, copy, conditional overwrite | `501`. |

The `ObjectStore` conformance suite of the CAS service gains a tierstore target; it proves create-only semantics, `412` on different bytes, range and HEAD behaviour, and that a LIST of a prefix returns exactly the live keys after PUTs and DELETEs (including across a node restart and with one node down).

**ETag.** The ETag is the 128-bit blake3 generation in hex, not an MD5; a client that verifies ETag as MD5 of the body is not supported (the CAS service does not).

### 10.10 Migration off silo (j)
Silo stays up, read-only after the flip, for the window. On the LATER profile **nothing in the new store shares a drive with silo** (the storage nodes are new hardware). On the NOW profile (section 21) the tier's cold directories sit on silo's own drives, and the fill limit counts silo's bytes (10.4); silo uses about 9.3 TB of the 96 TB raw, so the tier has room, and silo's removal (10.11 step 4) returns its space. What is left is the copy volume and the rollback cost. Steps, each reversible until the last, with the cost of each reversal stated:
1. **Bring the store up dark** (T-S tasks, T0 on the storage nodes): format the drives (a held script per node), write the membership document, start the daemons. Nothing writes to it; T0 and T0-F-b measure it.
2. **Add the legacy upstream** to the engine: `store.legacy_upstream` is silo's S3 endpoint, **read-only**. A fill tries the store first (`LOCATE`, 10.6) and reads silo when the store has a **definitive miss**; a non-definitive miss never imports (a rebooting node never starts a burst of copies). The part-grid rule for silo-origin packs (`upload_part_size`, 4.4) applies on that path only. Cost per fill: one `LOCATE` round trip before the silo GET; the engine remembers "legacy" for `store.legacy_hint_ttl` (10 minutes) so later fills of the pack on that node go straight to silo. Metric `legacy_reads_total{outcome}`.
3. **Import the live packs (decision: both mechanisms, in this order).** (a) *Bulk mover:* the CAS service, which knows every live pack, drives a throttled, resumable mover over live packs not yet in the store: read from silo as one stream, PUT through the S3 front (the writer node computes the generation), verify against cell digests where an L2 list exists, else against size and silo's own checksum; `import.bytes_per_hour` (default 200 GiB) and one stream per storage node, backing off on the store's 503. Dead packs are never copied. The live set is 1.34 TB [M]; at the default rate the pass is about 7 hours, and it enters through the hot tier (4.0 TB of NVMe at `hot.replicas = 3`, inside the 2.6 TB logical, 7.7 TB of NVMe, between the start and reject marks, 10.5), then destages as sealed blocks. (b) *Lazy import:* a definitive miss served from silo sends an import request to the pack's writer node, which reads the whole pack from silo once and PUTs it; duplicates converge as `identical`. (c) *Age out:* the CAS service condemns and deletes packs as trees expire; DELETEs go to the store and to silo.
4. **Flip the CAS service's writes** to the store's S3 front (config); every new pack is one streaming PUT to the store. **Rollback of this step is not free:** every tree published after the flip names packs that exist only in the store, so a rolled-back CAS service, which reads silo, finds none of them: each such tree is a miss (republish or rebuild), a cold start of every tree published since the flip. The runbook (T-S11) reads the scale from `store_puts_total` since the flip and sets the point after which rollback is not offered. No reverse mover is built.
5. **Catch-up pass** over live packs written to silo between the bulk pass and the flip, then **measure the end:** when `legacy_reads_total` has been 0 for 7 days and the mover reports nothing left, go to 10.11.

### 10.11 Retiring silo
Held scripts (dry-run by default, never forcing):
1. Confirm preconditions: `legacy_reads_total` zero for 7 days; **the repair of milestone S1 is running** (hot re-replication, cold-copy re-replication after a drive or node loss, scrub of hot blocks and cold copies, and the margin and live-key audits; 18): `replicas = 2` without repair would leave a block with one copy forever after its first drive failure, so silo's data is not destroyed before the store can restore its own copies; a scrub pass has covered every cold copy at least once; the live-key audit (10.8) over the CAS service's whole live list reports no unreadable key and no key missing from the store; `hot_blocks_by_copies` and the margin histogram show nothing below tolerance.
2. Remove `store.legacy_upstream`; this is the **last reversible step** (silo's data is intact; re-adding the upstream restores the fallback).
3. Stop and disable the silo service on each of its nodes through hostkeeper, one node at a time under the one-live-update coordinator.
4. **Destructive, held script:** remove silo's data from each drive after a final listing is archived; remove the silo package and unit from the node image.
5. Delete the `upload_part_size` handling, the legacy upstream and the silo client (T11b).

### 10.12 Why not SeaweedFS, why not Ceph, and why not silo (i)
Evidence: the SeaweedFS source (read at `97f49571`, and `weed/storage/erasure_coding/ec_encoder.go` re-read on the current main for this revision), its wiki and releases page, the Ceph erasure-code documentation, and silo's measured layout on the farm; nothing was run against SeaweedFS or Ceph. Labels: SOURCE read in code, WIKI read in docs, [E] estimate, [I] inference.

**A correction.** Revision 5 rejected SeaweedFS because "its erasure coding runs only on sealed volumes and its garbage collection fights a store that is deleted from", and the layout comparison of earlier drafts implied our contiguous blocks were an advantage. Both are withdrawn. **SeaweedFS's erasure coding already uses 1 GB contiguous blocks** (`ec_encoder.go`: `DataShardsCount = 10`, `ParityShardsCount = 4`, `ErasureCodingLargeBlockSize = 1GB`, a 1 MB small block for the tail; SOURCE): a `.dat` volume is consumed in rows of ten 1 GB blocks, so a needle read normally touches one shard, exactly the f4 behaviour this design has. Sealed-only encoding is also what this design does (it encodes sealed blocks), and deletes are rare today (0.035%). **The reasons to build our own are these five, and only these:**
1. **The 3-node geometry.** The open-source code path is fixed at 10+4 by default; 14 shards over 3 nodes are 5 + 5 + 4, so losing a node loses 5 shards against 4 parity: the code cannot survive its own failure domain on our hardware. (The current main source also carries a per-volume shard count for a configured, "custom-ratio" layout, and the wiki states custom ratios are an Enterprise feature; this was read in the source and the wiki, not verified against a specific release. Reasons 1 and 3 depend on that fact: with a configurable `4+4` they would not apply. Reasons 2, 4 and 5 stand with any ratio, so this design does not rest on the licensing fact.)
2. **Automatic destage and promote-on-read.** SeaweedFS encodes by a manual `ec.encode` shell command (or an admin or cron worker) on volumes at least 95% full and quiet for an hour (`shell/command_ec_encode.go`, SOURCE), on 30 GiB volumes; EC volumes are read-only ("Update is not supported", WIKI), so a cold object that is hot again cannot be promoted, and `volume.tier.move` moves whole volumes. Here destage is automatic on seal, decoupled from reclaim, and promotion copies one object into a cache segment (10.5).
3. **Repair-read traffic.** Rebuilding a lost shard reads 10 shards at the fixed 10+4 and 4 at `4+4` or `4+2`: about 2.5 times less repair traffic on the drives and the network [E] (with a custom ratio of `4+4`, the same).
4. **No per-object filer row.** The S3 gateway requires the filer, with one entry per object and its chunk list in leveldb2 (one filer) or an external database (SOURCE); 1.39 M objects are 1.39 M rows today, and an S3 PUT is cut into 8 MB chunks assigned to volumes independently (configurable; the hop count through filer, master lookup and volume server remains). Here the index is keyed by object and rebuildable from the blocks (10.2), and a pack is one record in one block.
5. **Integration.** One daemon and one configuration with tierstore's own cache, S3 front and fetch engine: `LOCATE` returns generation and location in one step, every response carries the drive's queue and state for the per-drive budget of 4.3, demand preemption reaches the drive (10.7), create-only is decided by the index under a key lock rather than a separate lock service (SeaweedFS answers 503 when its lock service is unavailable, SOURCE, on the path that serves 74% of PUT requests today [M]).

**Where SeaweedFS is stronger, and what we take on.** It is production software with frequent releases, tooling (`volume.fix.replication`, `ec.rebuild`, balance, an admin UI) and Prometheus metrics; its Haystack small-object read is one seek with about 16 bytes per needle in memory (SOURCE); its S3 gateway, LIST and conditional PUT exist today. Its releases also show data-path bugs (a `volume.fsck -volumeId` purge of live chunks, fixed in 4.52); that is a fact about a fast-moving project, not a verdict. **Building our own costs more:** section 18 plans about 95 agent-days (a range of 65 to 115, the upper half more likely) [E], and storage engines usually take two to three times a first estimate. The store keeps the engine's `Upstream` abstraction, so a build that slips can fall back to an S3-compatible store (SeaweedFS with replication `001` behind the same engine and the same S3 conformance suite) by configuration; T-S0 runs that conformance suite against a single-node `weed server -s3` first, because it costs hours.

**Ceph RGW, the baseline that can express this shape today.** A replicated pool on the three NVMe devices and an EC `4+4` pool on the HDDs with a custom CRUSH rule (3 hosts, up to 3 OSDs per host), RGW storage classes with a lifecycle transition from the NVMe class to the HDD class, and RGW's LIST, range GET and conditional PUT: that is this design's tiering, built from existing parts [I: read in the Ceph documentation, not run]. It is rejected for these reasons, each a property of how Ceph lays bytes out, not of its maturity:
- **Striped EC layout.** An RGW object in an EC pool is striped across the `k` data OSDs in stripe units of 4 KiB to 4 MiB (the profile's `stripe_unit`), so a healthy read of one pack touches four drives and waits for the slowest; this design reads one drive per object (10.4). The small-object reads that dominate by count would also touch four drives each.
- **Small-object amplification.** BlueStore on HDD with a 4 KiB `min_alloc_size` and `4+4` stores each chunk of a small object on eight OSDs with their own allocation and metadata; 99% of our objects are under 64 KiB [M]. Here they are records in a segment, 16-byte aligned.
- **No whole-block seal-to-cold.** A lifecycle transition copies objects one by one into the EC pool; nothing seals a 1 GiB block and encodes it as a unit with offsets preserved, so a transition rewrites per-object metadata and the HDDs see per-object writes.
- **Operational weight.** Monitors, managers, OSDs per drive (45 plus 3) and RGW daemons, a CRUSH map and placement-group tuning for a three-node cluster, against one daemon per node.
What is genuinely new here, and therefore must be built and tested: the per-stripe code recorded in every shard footer, seal-to-cold with record offsets preserved (so destage changes no index entry), and object-granular promotion. What is reused, not reinvented: Reed-Solomon (`reed-solomon-simd`), crc32c, blake3, the embedded ordered store (`fjall`), and the published designs of section References (Haystack, f4, Windows Azure Storage).

**Why not silo.** Silo is the farm's current store (MinIO lineage); nothing measured shows a defect in it, so **staying on it is the null hypothesis**. The reasons to leave it are the hardware and the shape, not a bug:
1. **Hot and cold tiers with different redundancy are not silo's model.** It is one erasure set per pool; there is no replicated fast tier in front of an erasure-coded one inside the system, no seal, no promote.
2. **Its set is fixed at format time and cannot be drained node by node**; the new nodes cannot join the old set, and 45 drives would be several independent sets.
3. **A GET fans out over a set.** At 8+4 each 1 MiB erasure block is eight 128 KiB shard runs on eight drives, so a GET waits for the slowest of eight queues and each drive does small runs [M geometry; the measured 36% efficiency of one drive at 1 MiB scattered reads shows the cost]. The design reads one stream from one drive.
4. **Small objects are one erasure stripe each**, 1.39 M of them on hard drives, against records in a 1 GiB segment (10.3); silo also keeps about 10 small writes per second on every drive when idle [M].
5. We would keep maintaining a MinIO-lineage binary ourselves.
Against that: silo works today, needs no new code and has no migration risk. That is the case for the phased delivery of 10.13 and for the fallback above.

### 10.13 Risks named
- **A new correctness surface, bigger than revision 5's.** The replication protocol (ballots, the majority seal, seal-and-roll, the ack rule), the cluster stamp and the liveness rule, destage's atomic descriptor commit, the stripe-free protocol, the index and its recovery, tombstones, relocate, rebuild and the degraded read are all new code that can lose, duplicate or resurrect data. f4's authors name journal-based delete tracking "the foremost source of production issues": the tombstone log is the same shape. Mitigations: write-once blocks, content-derived generations, one ordering rule (newest stamp wins) for records, relocates and tombstones, a crc on every record and cell, the stripe descriptor in every shard footer, deterministic simulation of every protocol under scripted crashes, symmetric and asymmetric partitions, lost acknowledgements and reorderings (section 14), and the audit and scrub counters (10.8, 10.4).
- **Estimate risk.** About 95 agent-days, range 65 to 115 with the upper half more likely (section 18), is a planning figure, not a measurement; storage engines run two to three times a first estimate, and the line-rate calibration of 3 k lines per agent-day comes from plumbing PRs. **Phased delivery limits the exposure:** milestone S1 ships the hot tier, the cold tier with `replicas = N` (no erasure coding), the S3 front with LIST, the repair that `replicas = N` needs (re-replication, scrub of copies, the margin and live-key audits) and the migration; S2 adds erasure coding, its rebuild and deep scrub; S3 adds promote and GC rewrite. Silo can retire after S1 if the replicated cold tier fits the capacity (at `replicas = 2` the cold tier is 114.8 TB logical, 71.4 TB under the guard, against 1.34 TB live).
- **Three nodes are few.** With `4+2` a node down leaves zero parity; with `4+4` one spare; with `hot.replicas = 3` and `min_acks = 2`, un-destaged data is on two NVMe devices at acknowledgement and on three once the third copy lands, so it survives a node plus another NVMe; a record acknowledged on two and lost on both before the third copy lands (two simultaneous NVMe losses inside the replication lag) is the remaining window (Q2). The NVMe is a single device per node, so its loss is the node's loss for hot data (10.3). Rolling updates take one node down at a time under the coordinator, so the exposure is the length of a node's update; `rebuild.node_down_after` keeps an update from starting a rebuild. A minority partition cannot seal or acknowledge; it answers 503 (10.3).
- **Drive facts unknown.** CMR or SMR, rpm and rate of the SAS drives, the NVMe's sustained write, `fdatasync` time and power-loss protection, link speed and RAM are all T0 outputs; every constant marked [I] changes with them, and an NVMe without PLP stops the group-commit defaults for the owner's decision (10.3). Sealed sequential writes and whole-file unlink are the safe choice on SMR as on CMR.
- **Degraded exposure.** A node down degrades about a third of cold reads for its duration and cuts cold read capacity to about 2.8 GB/s (4.3); the budget charges `k` per degraded part, the hedge (4.6) bounds the tail, the margin-0 rules of 10.4 apply, and the audit (10.8) shows the margin. There is no read drain: the data shards of a down node cannot be read anywhere else.
- **Index size and recovery time.** The index lives in an embedded ordered store on the NVMe (10.2), so its memory is the store's cache, not one entry per key in RAM. Recovery replays from the replay mark in seconds to minutes; a full rebuild from the cold trailers is read-heavy (10.2). T-S3 measures bytes per key, point-read latency and bulk load at 1e8 keys; if `fjall` fails them, `redb` replaces it behind the same trait.
- **Partial stripes and fragmentation.** Expiry classes multiply open segments and stripes waiting for `k` blocks; a partial stripe costs up to 5x at `4+4` (10.5). The design bounds it (one open segment per replica pair and class, the age-seal floor, stripes from any pair grouped by length, first-fit decreasing packing), and `cold_fill_efficiency`, `gc.classes_max`, `partial_after` and the counters show it.
- **GC and garbage are not measured.** 38% of pack bytes are dead today and no GC runs; the rewrite cost and the effect of expiry grouping are estimates until T0-F C-10 replays the death distribution (10.5).
- **Promote policy is unvalidated.** Thresholds are guesses; with 0.5 MB/s of cluster reads it will mostly idle (10.5).
- **Rollback after the flip** costs a cold start of every tree published since (10.10).
- **Coupling with the CAS service's deletes.** The store with a cold tier never evicts for capacity, so a pack disappears only by a DELETE or by loss beyond the code's tolerance; a running job whose pack the CAS service deletes takes the lost path of 4.6, and the CAS service's 404 handling (3.5) is a requirement.
- **Hot spots.** A hot pack is one block on one spindle; defences are the 31 node caches, hedged degraded reads, the per-drive backoff of 4.3, slot release (10.7) and promote-on-read.

**Hot-only mode: no cold tier [D].** The same daemon and code, without destage: `cold.mode = "none"`. The hot tier is then the durable tier, its redundancy is `hot.replicas`, and there is no dirty-versus-clean distinction. Segments still seal (so reclaim and GC work on whole files; `hot.seal_bytes` defaults to 1023 MiB and `cold.block_bytes`, 1 GiB, still bounds it) and the index, stamps, majority seals, tombstones, LIST and the S3 subset are unchanged. **The marks of 10.5 are measured on each node's total hot bytes** (open and sealed segments and large files, as a fraction of its NVMe) instead of dirty bytes. What happens when the NVMe fills is the one choice, `hot.on_full`:
- `"reject"`: never drop data. Above `dirty_slow` PUTs answer 503 `Retry-After` and above `dirty_reject` 507, exactly as a stalled destage would; GC (deletes, compaction of mostly dead segments) is the only way space returns. This is a store.
- `"evict"`: cache semantics. When any member of a replica set is above `dirty_destage_start` (members report their fill in the ping), the set's writer drops the set's least recently read **sealed** segments and large files, whole, until every member is below `dirty_destage_stop`; open segments are never dropped. A drop is a **block-drop** record keyed by the block id or the large file's name: it applies to the records physically in that block or file only, never to the key (so a copy of the key in another block is untouched). It is replicated to every node like a tombstone, a returning node pulls it like a tombstone, and the record itself is dropped only when every node not `out` has unlinked the file. Every copy unlinks the file and removes the index entries pointing into it. A later read of a dropped key is a miss (404, a definitive miss), and a build node takes the lost path of 4.6. If writes outrun eviction so that open segments alone pass `dirty_reject`, PUTs answer 507. This is a cache, and `store_evicted_bytes_total` counts it.
Validation refuses `hot.on_full` when a cold tier exists, refuses `cold.mode = "none"` without `hot.on_full`, and in hot-only mode refuses the cold-only keys (`cold.ec`, `cold.replicas`, `cold.drives` and the other `cold.*` keys except `mode`, `block_bytes` and `block_reserve`; `destage.*`; `promote.*`; `hot.keep_clean`; `hot.promo_share`; `gc.rewrite_fill`).

**Validation at startup (a configuration that cannot survive its own declared failure is refused, not warned about):**
1. `hot.replicas` is at most the number of hot failure domains; `hot.min_acks` is between 1 and `replicas`.
2. With `cold.mode = "tiered"`: exactly one of `cold.ec` and `cold.replicas`. For EC: `k + m` is at most the number of cold drives, and with `failure_domain = "node"`: `max_shards_per_domain >= ceil((k + m) / domains)` and **`m >= max_shards_per_domain`**; with `"drive"`, `m >= 1`. For replicas: `replicas` is at most the number of domains. With `cold.mode = "none"`: no `ec`, `replicas` or `drives`, and `hot.on_full` set.
3. `hot.seal_bytes <= cold.block_bytes - cold.block_reserve`; `hot.large_min <= cold.block_bytes / 4`; `hot.seal_min_bytes <= hot.seal_bytes`.
4. The marks are ordered: `dirty_destage_stop < dirty_destage_start < dirty_urgent < dirty_slow < dirty_reject <= 1`; `cold.fill_limit < cold.rebuild_fill_ceiling < 1`.
5. `destage.min_age <= destage.max_age`; `gc.rewrite_below` in (0, 1).
6. A proxy configuration (configurations A and C of tierstore-design.md) refuses `store.kind = "tiered"` as its cold store.
7. The code is recorded per stripe: changing `cold.ec` changes new stripes only; the running code of each stripe is in its descriptor, and old stripes are converted only by the background re-encode (10.4).

## 11. The cache device: a real partition, not a loop on an image
**Decision [D]: a dedicated xfs partition of the node SSD (noatime), no loop device. Required, not gated:** T0 measured that the loop device turns `O_DIRECT` cache writes into page-cache writes (945+ MB/s that have not reached the SSD) and stalls four buffered writers for more than 20 s [M]. The other reasons stand:
1. Each `fdatasync` through xfs-on-loop-on-image costs two journals and two flushes.
2. Two page caches and two dirty accountings double memory pressure (`losetup --direct-io=on` was the considered stopgap; with `O_DIRECT` already defeated by the loop it is not relied on).
3. Cell-group eviction frees space only if discards propagate through the loop to the image; on a partition `PUNCH_HOLE` frees directly and `fstrim` works.
4. A fixed 185 GiB image takes the space whether or not the cache is full.
5. One fewer layer between FUSE reads and the SSD.

The device is the build node's local SSD that holds the cache (on the farm's six existing nodes the root SATA SSD; their SMR drives carry only silo until it is retired and are never a cache): a new partition of budget + 15% (185 GiB -> 220 GiB). The 31 build nodes of the target farm have their own cache SSDs, whose size and type T0 records. **If the root filesystem is xfs it cannot be shrunk; T0 records its type.** The plan is free unallocated SSD space if it exists, else a node image re-provision through hostkeeper in a maintenance window; never an in-place live repartition by tierstore.

**T0 B** measures the partition (it is no longer a gate): `fdatasync` p99, sustained write at 1 MiB and mixed-load read p99, against the loop numbers above. **T10b depends on T13 so constants are frozen on the shipped device.** The partition is mounted at a new path read from `cache.root`; the old image is not converted and is removed after the soak.

## 13. Deleted code and migration of the cache
**Deleted**

- `pin_once`, `pinning`, `lifecycle`, `loaded.pinned`, touch/pin/unpin/pins_live traffic.
- All three prefetch mechanisms: `local_cache/prefetch.rs`, `fs-prefetch` threads, Prepare groups and `GROUP_MAX_BYTES`.
- Extents machinery: `local_cache/extents/*`, per-range flights, `extent_save`, `extents.writing`, `move_whole`'s re-hash, `fetch_parts` gap reading.
- The v1 list reader (only refusal remains) and, after the dual-emission release, the CAS service's v1 list writer.
- The komira-ai/fuser fork dependency and the blocking mount client (T-R, T11a).
- The erasure-set budget model and the `upstream.data_shards`/`upstream.drives` topology flags (T11a); at silo's retirement, the silo client as a general upstream, the legacy upstream and the `upload_part_size` handling (T-S11, T11b).
- Config: `max_concurrent_fills`, `fill_socket_concurrency`, `prefetch_concurrency`, `trees.fill_concurrency`, `fill_chunk`, `trees.fill_queue`, `trees.fill_rate`; `trees.coalesce_gap` and `trees.whole_pack_percent` replaced by section 6.

**Kept:** the reserve and critical-mark check (at claim time), staging files and create-only link for non-pack objects, lock-generation `owns()` checks, the S3-front index, tree image serving (komira-ai/tierstore#341, komira-ai/tierstore#342), tree store placements and the provider Prepare entry point, the NFS front on macOS.

**Cache layout migration.** It is a cache; there is no conversion. The new binary writes `packs-v2/`, does not read `extents-v1/` or `origin=span` entries, and deletes them in a low-priority `tierstore gc legacy`. **Rollback:** the old binary ignores `packs-v2/`; during the overlap `packs-v2` is capped at 50% of the budget and the rollback procedure includes `tierstore gc packs-v2 --purge` (a held script) before the old binary starts. List-format rollback is in 3.5; store rollback is in 10.10.

## 16. Farm measurement plan (T0, T0-F and T0-S, before tuning)
Farm targets, never on a developer machine. On a build node, run on **one node drained of jobs** (no mounts, no Prepare) while the jobscheduler holds admission on the others; on a storage node, run before it holds data, or on drives that carry no data (scratch files go in `ts-scratch` directories on the drives' xfs, never in `ts-hot` or `ts-cold` of a live drive, never on silo's data). Before each run, record `iostat -x` of the drives involved for 60 s and abort if util > 5%. Each cell: 20 s after a 5 s warm-up, page cache dropped between cells; output MB/s, p50, p99, `iostat -x` (`rareq-sz`, `r_await`, `aqu-sz`, `util`). **Nothing here loads a kernel module or changes firewall or socket state on a node.**

## 17. Benchmark plan
The benchmarks below compare candidate FUSE libraries (fuser, fuse3/rfuse3, polyfuse, fuse-backend-rs) with `tsfuse`, and the storage tier with silo. Results are published with their raw data and configuration. Numbers come from **one farm node**, one harness throughout.

### 17.1 FUSE layer
**Harness.** `ts-fs-open-stall-load`: N open/read/close processes over a real komira tree (same tree and file list for every variant) plus CPU burners (one per core), 60 s per run, **at least 5 runs per variant, median and min/max**, first run discarded. Node drained. Per run record kernel, `fuse` module parameters, CPU governor and count, mount options, `entry_timeout`/`attr_timeout`, `max_read`/`max_write`, open mode, reader count, library commit. Harness and raw data live in a bench crate; no shell.

**Variants.** V1 fuser fork as shipped in 0.3.119 (baseline 2,769 opens/s, p50 74 ms, p99 312 ms; re-run on the day). V2 fork plus the pin fix of komira-ai/tierstore#343 (28,199 opens/s, p50 7.7 ms, p99 26 ms; re-run). V3 `tsfuse` `/dev/fuse`, 1 reader and N cloned readers (N in {2, 4, 8, cores/4}), writev replies. V4 same with splice replies. V5 `tsfuse` over io_uring, default depth and larger. V6 (optional, only if each costs under a day) a minimal read-only filesystem on fuse3 (rfuse3) and fuse-backend-rs over the same mapped list; polyfuse and the rest are a **static feature table** (async dispatch, clone, splice, uring, resend, copy count per read) read from source at pinned commits; unread cells stay empty.

**Two regimes, both reported.** (a) Library regime: `entry_timeout=0`, `attr_timeout=0`, explicit OPEN each time (the shipped baselines' settings). (b) Production regime: long timeouts and `NO_OPEN`. A **null filesystem** isolates transport from tierstore logic; V3-V5 run with both.

**Metrics.** opens/s; GETATTR and OPEN latency p50, p99, max (library histograms and client side); read MB/s cold and warm; **CPU per 1k requests** (server `utime+stime`, cgroup kernel time, whole-node delta); **D-state and FUSE waiting under a real komira build** (named targets): `fuse_d_state_threads`, `/sys/fs/fuse/connections/<n>/waiting`, D-state counts, per-request latency histogram, build wall time and FUSE-wait fraction; for V5 `uring_entries_parked` and `uring_registered_bytes` under a cold-read storm of `depth x queues x 1.5` and metadata p99 during it.

**Decision rules (before the data).** io_uring is default only if it improves CPU per 1k requests or p99 by at least 20% against `/dev/fuse` with clones, does not regress cold or warm read MB/s by more than 5%, survives the storm without metadata p99 growth, **fits 64 MiB of registered memory per mount**, and satisfies the takeover requirement (5.2 item 5). Splice replaces `pread`+`writev` above a threshold only where it wins on CPU per 1k requests at equal MB/s. DONTNEED stays on only if warm MB/s does not fall and page cache falls. The number of cloned readers is the knee of opens/s versus CPU; the shared pending queue (5.2) predicts a knee.

Raw CSVs and exact configuration are published with the result tables.

**Which tasks start now, and which wait for this revision's formats.** Start now: T0-F-a, T0 (build nodes), T-F0, T-B1, T-ENV, T-FMT Q1, T-K1a, and the client-side tasks (T-F*, T1*, T2 to T9), none of which depend on the store's formats; the first half of T0-S as soon as the storage nodes exist (drive facts, NVMe `fdatasync` and PLP, network, RAM, encode and decode MB/s including a parity-shard rebuild). T-I freezes the store's record (stamp), tombstone, block, footer, seal and free formats and the replication messages (ballot, stamp) as written here; then T-S0, T-S1, T-S2 and T-S5 in parallel, and the rest in the order above.

## 20. Limits of this design
- **Nothing here was run.** [I], [E] and [K] items have named checks: T0-S and T0-F (drive behaviour, CMR or SMR, scheduler constants, network, encode speed, object sizes, GC replay), T-F0 (kernel behaviour), T-FMT Q1 (tree id).
- **The owner's decisions** are at the top: the hardware and the tier shapes, Q1 the cold code (`4+4`, decided), Q2 hot replicas (3, decided), Q3 per-cell digests (default yes, sha256), Q4 the old-tree trust gap.
- **The CAS service's decisions (T-K1a):** its 404 handling (the miss path), which the lost path of 4.6 relies on, and whether it sends the expiry-class hint.
- **Three storage nodes are a small cluster.** The tolerance is one node at a time (one spare shard at `4+4`, none at `4+2`); a second fault during a node outage or during the destage window loses data that is not yet protected twice. A minority partition can neither seal nor acknowledge. The design states the windows (10.13) and measures the margin (10.8); it does not claim more.
- **Create-only is linearizable only while the storage nodes' views agree**; a divergence is detected and counted, not prevented (10.5). With content-derived keys it converges.
- **Durability of hot data is `hot.replicas` copies until destage**; with `min_acks` below `replicas` it is weaker, as documented (10.3).
- **GC is designed and not measured.** Rewrite costs, the effect of expiry grouping and the promote thresholds are estimates until T0-F C-10 and C-3b.
- **The estimate is about 95 agent-days, 65 to 115 with the upper half more likely,** with a calendar of about 7 to 10 weeks to the flip (S1) and 4 to 6 more for S2 and S3 [E]. Silo is the null hypothesis; the fallback to an S3-compatible store (10.12) is kept available by the `Upstream` abstraction.
- **Rollback of the CAS service's write flip** costs a cold start of every tree published since the flip (10.10).
- **io_uring FUSE** (parked entries, memory, hybrid delivery, takeover) is decided by T-F0, T-F5 and the section 17 rules; `/dev/fuse` is the default until they pass. `fuse.enable_uring` is set at boot by the hostkeeper image.
- **Kernel facts tagged [K]** (reply matching per device, resend with clones, `F_SEAL_FUTURE_WRITE`, uring INTERRUPT delivery, `F_PUNCHHOLE` on APFS) come from source knowledge; each has a T-F0, T-F7 or T-M1 check. The farm kernel version is reported, not verified.
- The CAS service's hashing CPU roughly doubles (cell digests); T-FMT measures it. The id-preserving backfill reads packs once; its cost per TiB is estimated in T-FMT before it runs.
- **On the NOW profile the tier shares the SMR drives with silo,** whose I/O it cannot schedule, so demand first-byte targets are weaker there until silo is retired (21.1).
- The 60 ms cold first-byte figure is an idle-farm target; the loaded bound of 10.7 is a hypothesis for T10b.
- The first-part-small recipe, the P probe arm, striping across hot replicas, the write share, slot release, object-granular promote, expiry-class grouping and the per-stripe code in every shard footer are this design's own, with no external source; T0-S and T10b validate them. The majority seal is single-decree Paxos and the cluster stamp is a hybrid logical clock (References); their use here is new code with simulation tests, not a library.
- **The cold read capacity with a node down (about 2.8 GB/s) and the rebuild times are estimates** from vendor drive rates; T0-S and 17.2 measure them.

## 21. Deployment profiles [D]
One codebase, one binary and one configuration schema (10.14) serve both deployments. A profile is only configuration and a membership document; nothing in the code branches on which profile runs. The store's formats are the same in both, and the code of every stripe is in its descriptor, so the migration from NOW to LATER moves bytes and changes configuration and membership, never code or formats.

### 21.1 NOW: six combined nodes on today's hardware
**Hardware [M]:** six nodes, each with 2 x 8 TB drive-managed SMR hard drives (ST8000DM004, xfs, which also hold silo during the migration) and one SATA SSD of about 1 TB (root filesystem today; the node cache moves to a partition of it, section 11). There is no NVMe. Each node runs builds **and** stores packs: `role = "combined"` (10.1).

**Hot tier: a partition of the SATA SSD, `hot.replicas = 2`, `min_acks = 2`.** The SSD is the only fast device. The hot tier gets its own xfs partition beside the cache partition (sized by T0 from the SSD's free space; this profile assumes 300 GiB [I]), so the two never share free space or a filesystem journal. Why two replicas, not three as on LATER:
- the SSD also holds the root filesystem and the build cache, so every extra copy is write load on the device the jobs read from; three copies of 34 GB/day is about 100 GB/day of SATA writes spread over six SSDs, which is affordable, but buys little here;
- with six failure domains, two copies already survive any one node, and the short destage window below bounds the time data spends on two devices;
- the consumer SATA SSDs have no power-loss protection [I, T0 records it], so each group commit is a real flash program (milliseconds [I]); at about 2.5 small PUTs per second that is acceptable, and fewer copies keep the tail short. The stop condition of 10.3 applies to the LATER NVMe only; on NOW the rate is low enough that T0's `fdatasync` latency sets `batch_max` and nothing else.
`LOCATE` asks the first live node of `rank(K)` (10.6); a hot read crosses the network five times in six, at SATA SSD speed, which is not the bottleneck: the co-located build cache absorbs repeats.

**Cold tier: `ec = 4+2` on the 12 SMR drives, failure domain node, one shard per node, `max_shards_per_domain = 2`.** Twelve drives on six nodes allow a code of 6 shards with one per node. `4+2` at one shard per node survives the loss of **any two nodes**, or of a node and a drive, at 1.5x raw; after a node is lost for good its stripes are rebuilt onto the five survivors with one survivor holding two shards (allowed, since `m = 2 >= 2`), so the cluster again survives a further node. Against the alternatives:
- `replicas = 2` costs 2.0x and survives only one node;
- `8+4` at two shards per node is also 1.5x and survives two nodes, but a stripe waits for 8 sealed blocks (more partial stripes at 34 GB/day, 10.5), a repair reads 8 shards instead of 4, and every stripe touches every drive;
- `4+4` would cost 2.0x for a tolerance six domains do not need.
`4+2` also keeps repair reads at 4, the same as LATER. On SMR drives the tier's write pattern, whole sequential shard files and whole-file unlinks, is the pattern drive-managed SMR handles well, and one healthy read is one stream on one drive [M: T0 measured 119 MB/s sequential and 36% of that at 1 MiB scattered on these drives]. C-5b (steady-state churn) runs in full on NOW because the drives are SMR.

**Sharing the drives with silo.** The tier's cold directories are `ts-cold/` on silo's xfs filesystems. Fill is measured on the filesystem (10.4), so silo's bytes count: `0.85 x (96 TB - one node's 16 TB - one drive's 8 TB)` is 61.2 TB of filesystem use under the guard (`reserve_drives = 1`), of which silo holds about 9.3 TB [M], leaving about 51.9 TB raw, **about 34.6 TB logical at `4+2`**, against 1.34 TB live [E]. The drive scheduler cannot see silo's I/O (about 10 small writes per second per drive [M]) except through its own service times, which is why demand first-byte targets are weaker on NOW; the per-drive budget of 4.3 backs off on those times as on any slow drive.

### 21.2 LATER: three storage nodes and 31 build nodes
**Hardware [D]:** 3 storage nodes of 1 x 6.4 TB U.2 NVMe and 15 x 6 TB SAS HDD; 31 build nodes with local SSD caches. **Hot tier: the NVMe, `hot.replicas = 3`, `min_acks = 2`** (Q2: every storage node holds every hot object; a hot read is served by the least-loaded node without a hop). **Cold tier: `ec = 4+4` at 3/3/2, failure domain node, `reserve_drives = 2`** (Q1). The storage node's configuration is the first example of 10.14; a build node's is `role = "build"` with the membership document and its cache. Capacity is in 10.15.

### 21.3 Migration from NOW to LATER, without a rewrite
Each step is a membership or configuration change of the same binary; each is reversible until the last.
1. **Bring up the storage nodes dark:** format their drives (a held script per node), T0-S, start the daemons with the LATER storage configuration in a membership epoch where they are present and **`in`**, while the combined nodes stay `in`. Their indexes start empty and are filled by `index-sync` from the combined nodes; until then they answer `indexing`.
2. **Move new writes:** a new membership epoch marks every combined node's hot device and drives **`drain`**: they are no longer placement targets for hot replica sets or new stripes, but they keep serving reads. Replica sets are computed over nodes whose hot device is `in`, so from this epoch every PUT goes to the three storage nodes with `hot.replicas = 3`, and every new stripe is `4+4` on them. (The cluster-wide hot settings, `replicas = 3` and `min_acks = 2`, take effect in the same epoch.)
3. **Drain the hot tier of the combined nodes:** each seals its open segments (the majority seal of 10.3 now runs over all live nodes) and destages every dirty block onto the storage nodes' drives as `4+4` stripes; clean and cached copies are dropped. Nothing is copied to the storage nodes' NVMe: those blocks are cold.
4. **Drain the cold tier of the combined nodes by re-encoding:** for each `4+2` stripe with shards on `drain` drives, a coordinator on a storage node reads its four data shards (one sequential stream per SMR drive), writes four data and four parity shards at 3/3/2 on the storage nodes, commits the new descriptor (the same majority commit as destage; block ids and offsets are unchanged, so no index entry moves), and then unlinks the six old shards. It is a `background` job at `rebuild.share`, verified like a destage. At today's volume (a few TB of cold data) it is hours [E].
5. **Change roles:** when no data remains on `drain` devices (`cold_stripes{...}` and `hot_dirty_bytes` on the combined nodes at zero), a final epoch marks the combined nodes' storage role `out` and their configuration becomes `role = "build"`; they join the 31 build nodes. Their SMR drives hold only silo (if silo has not been retired yet, 10.11) and are then freed.
**Rollback:** until step 5, marking the combined nodes `in` again restores them as placement targets; stripes already moved stay valid where they are, because a stripe's code and placement are in its descriptor. **Tests:** the migration is a scripted simulation in section 14 (the membership epochs above on fake nodes: reads succeed at every step, nothing is placed on `drain` devices, every stripe ends `4+4` on the storage nodes, no index entry changes during the re-encode).
